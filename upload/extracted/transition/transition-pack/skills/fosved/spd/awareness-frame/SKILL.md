# Awareness Frame — Reconnaissance Before Battle

Before the surgeon picks up the scalpel, the surgeon looks at the body. Where is it. What is its state. What is acting on it from outside. What is its remaining strength.

This is the first phase of any non-trivial analysis. It is short — 5 to 7 lines of work — but it changes everything that follows. Without it, deconstruction loses its target and the staff phase loses its ground. The 12 scalpels still produce data, but the data has no orientation.

Critical conceptual note: the **"I"** in this frame is **not the analyst**. It is the **subject under analysis** — the process, the event, the actor, the decision point being studied. We position the subject in its field of forces, not ourselves.

## Prerequisites

- A defined subject of analysis (clearly stated, not a vague topic)
- Understanding that this skill produces orientation, not conclusions
- Awareness that deeper depth levels (3+) may extend this frame to deep profiling

## Core principle

> The subject is not floating in vacuum. It sits in a specific field of forces, at a specific moment in macro and event time, with specific vulnerabilities and leverage points. Naming these explicitly is the difference between analysis that aims and analysis that scatters.

The classical mistake is to start cutting (Layer 1, Layer 2) without knowing what we are cutting and where it stands. This produces accurate but useless data. The awareness frame is a discipline against that — it forces a single coherent statement of position before any tool touches the subject.

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Identify the subject precisely

State the subject in one sentence. Not "Belarus" — too vague. "The political-economic structure of Lukashenko's regime in May 2026, considering succession risk." Not "Bitcoin" — too vague. "Bitcoin as a monetary asset class in the current debt-cycle phase, considering CBDC competition." Precision here saves all downstream work.

### Step 2 — Place subject in macro phase

Where in the long cycles is the subject? At minimum check three:

- **Debt cycle** (Dalio): early / mid / late / blow-off / reset
- **Geopolitical regime cycle**: hegemonic stability / contested / multi-polar transition / new order forming
- **Technological cycle** for relevant tech (if applicable): early / scaling / maturing / disrupted

Subject's behavior is constrained by the macro phase it occupies. A startup in 1995 vs 2025 — different game even at same valuation, because the macro phase differs.

### Step 3 — Place subject in event-time

Where in the immediate event sequence is the subject?
- Build-up phase (forces accumulating)
- Trigger/inflection point
- Acute phase (events unfolding rapidly)
- Resolution phase
- Post-event phase (consequences cascading)

Different phases require different analytical emphasis. Build-up needs trend analysis. Acute needs game theory. Post-event needs second-order consequence mapping.

### Step 4 — Map the field of forces around subject

What forces are acting **on** the subject right now? List them concretely (named actors, named pressures, named constraints). Don't say "international pressure" — say "EU sanctions package #14 + Russian fiscal demands + Chinese trade dependency." Three to seven named forces is the right count. Less = incomplete map. More = noise.

For each force, note its **direction** (compresses subject? supports subject? distorts subject?) and **rough magnitude** (low/medium/high).

### Step 5 — Map subject's outward potential

What forces can the subject exert **outward**? What does it have that affects others?

This is often skipped — analysts focus on what acts on subject, ignoring what subject acts on. But strategic options live here. A subject with no outward force is purely passive (rare). A subject with outward force has manoeuvring room.

### Step 6 — Identify vulnerabilities and leverage points

Two opposite questions:
- **Vulnerabilities:** where is subject brittle? what shocks would it not survive?
- **Leverage points:** where is subject sensitive? small input causing large output?

Vulnerabilities show where the subject can be hurt. Leverage points show where small actions (by subject or by others) cause disproportionate effects.

### Step 7 — Map environment & substrate

Subject does not float in vacuum. It grows in an environment that supplies (or withholds) what it needs to function. The tree analogy: even the strongest tree dies in poor soil; even a weak tree thrives in rich substrate.

For the subject, identify:

**Substrate (resources subject draws from):**
- Capital base (financial, human, political, informational)
- Energy / motive resources (literal energy if relevant; also social legitimacy, motivation)
- Time horizon available (subject with long horizon has different options than subject with short)
- Information access (what subject can know about its field)

For each, note: abundant / sufficient / depleting / depleted.

**Climate (macro environmental conditions):**
- What season is the subject in? (favorable phase / neutral / hostile phase)
- What weather is currently active? (calm / strong winds / storms)
- What is the climate trend? (becoming friendlier / stable / becoming hostile)

**External destructive forces (winds and storms):**
- What forces could break branches or strip leaves rapidly?
- Are any of these active now? Imminent?
- What is subject's resilience to each?

**Internal destructive processes (diseases and parasites):**
- This is the most-missed category. Internal processes can hollow out a subject from inside while external indicators look normal.
- Are there signs of institutional decay, talent erosion, ideological exhaustion, financial dependence on extraordinary measures?
- Full diagnosis is `system-health-diagnostics` skill (Tier 2). Awareness frame just flags whether triggers are present.

### Step 8 — Synthesize into 7-10 line frame

Compress all the above into compact frame. Example structure:

```
SUBJECT: [precise one-sentence definition]
MACRO PHASE: [debt cycle stage] / [geopolitical phase] / [tech phase if applicable]
EVENT PHASE: [where in immediate sequence]
FORCES INWARD: [3-5 named forces with direction and magnitude]
FORCES OUTWARD: [what subject can project]
VULNERABILITIES: [1-3 critical brittle points]
LEVERAGE POINTS: [1-3 high-sensitivity inputs]
SUBSTRATE: [resources status — abundant/sufficient/depleting/depleted, with notes]
CLIMATE: [season + active weather + trend]
INTERNAL HEALTH: [signs of disease/parasites if present, or "no triggers" if absent]
```

This compact summary is carried forward into the deconstruction phase as orientation. If `INTERNAL HEALTH` flags any triggers, `system-health-diagnostics` skill is automatically loaded for the subsequent deconstruction.

## Adaptive depth integration

At depth level 0-1, awareness frame is the compact 5-7 lines above. Done in 30 seconds.

At depth level 2-3, awareness frame is expanded:
- Macro phase positioning is supported with specific cycle indicators
- Forces inward and outward are quantified with confidence intervals
- Vulnerabilities and leverage points are tested against historical analogues

At depth level 4 (full intelligence operation), awareness frame includes:
- Deep profiling of decision-makers within subject (psychological state, recent statements, body language analysis from open-source video, network of personal influences, signs of stress or impairment)
- Intelligence on subject's recent strategic communications (internal leaks, off-record interviews, body language at public events)
- Cross-referencing subject's awareness of its own situation (does the subject know what it knows? — second-order awareness)

## Worked example

Subject: BYN/USD exchange rate trajectory through end of 2026.

```
SUBJECT: USD/BYN exchange rate trajectory from 2.83 (current, 2026-05-01) to 2026-12-31
MACRO PHASE: late-stage debt cycle for fiat / contested geopolitical / late-stage post-USSR economic dependency for Belarus
EVENT PHASE: post-stabilization (rate strengthened recent month after sustained weakness); awaiting Q2 fiscal data from Russia
FORCES INWARD:
  - Russian fiscal stress + emission risk (HIGH magnitude, weakens BYN with 1-3mo lag)
  - NBRB intervention capacity (MEDIUM, supports BYN tactically)
  - Belarusian population FX selling (MEDIUM, currently supportive)
  - Potash export prices (LOW-MEDIUM currently, supportive at $250+/ton)
  - EU sanctions + China trade (MEDIUM, mixed)
FORCES OUTWARD: minimal — BYN is rate-taker not rate-maker
VULNERABILITIES: any acute fiscal event in Russia → cascade with 1-3mo lag; any banking stress within Belarus
LEVERAGE POINTS: NBRB rate decisions sensitive 7-14 day window; potash price swing >$50/ton produces visible 30-day impact

SUBSTRATE:
- Capital: depleting — NBRB reserves drawing down despite official figures; private FX savings limited
- Energy/motive: sufficient — energy supply from Russia stable for now
- Time horizon: short — NBRB cannot run intervention beyond 6-9 months without external support
- Information access: limited — political constraints on accurate inflation reporting

CLIMATE:
- Season: hostile — late-stage debt cycle is unfavorable for satellite currencies pegged to a struggling primary
- Active weather: strong winds (Russian fiscal stress, war drag), low likelihood of sudden storms
- Trend: becoming hostile — ruble pressure increases over 12mo horizon

INTERNAL HEALTH:
- Signs present: NBRB independence questionable (multiple high-level changes 2024-25); reporting metrics increasingly diverging from market reality; growing reliance on administrative measures (FX restrictions)
- Load system-health-diagnostics for full diagnosis of NBRB and broader Belarusian fiscal apparatus
```

This 12-line frame, written before Layer 1, orients the entire analysis. The 12 layers will produce data, but they will produce it **for this subject in this position with these substrate constraints under these climate conditions with these health concerns**, not for "Belarus economy" in general.

## Anti-patterns

- **Putting the analyst in the frame.** The analyst's biases and limitations belong in calibration discipline, not in the awareness frame. Awareness is about subject's position, not analyst's position.
- **Vague macro phase.** "Uncertain times" is not a macro phase. Late-debt-cycle-with-disinflation-pressure is. If the macro phase cannot be named, more cycle work is needed before proceeding.
- **Missing outward forces.** Treating subject as purely passive even when it has projection capacity. Even small countries have some outward force; even individual actors do.
- **Conflating vulnerability with leverage.** Vulnerability = where subject can be hurt. Leverage = where small input creates large output. They sometimes overlap but are conceptually different. Mixing them produces unfocused analysis.
- **Producing more than 7 lines.** Awareness frame is short by design. Padding it loses the discipline. If 7 lines feel insufficient, the depth level may need to be higher (3+) — but the awareness frame itself stays compact, with deeper material going into specific layers.
- **Starting deconstruction without the frame.** Skipping awareness because "we know the topic" is exactly when this skill is most valuable — familiar topics carry inherited assumptions that the frame surfaces.

## Integration with deconstruction protocol

Awareness frame is **mandatory pre-step**. The deconstruction skill (`deconstruct`) requires it before Layer 1. Quality gate: deconstruction analysis without awareness frame is rejected — analyst must produce frame first, then proceed.

The frame is **carried forward** into all subsequent layers. Layer 6 (Context) builds on macro phase from frame. Layer 9 (Forces & Potentials) builds on inward/outward forces from frame. Layer 12 (Forecast) checks scenarios against vulnerabilities and leverage points from frame.

Frame is **archived** as part of every Analysis record (field `awarenessFrame` in Prisma). It is the subject's position-statement at time of analysis — useful for retrospective verification (was the subject correctly positioned? did the analyst miss something about the macro phase?).
