# Narrative vs Flows — Where The Words Stop, The Money Speaks

Public narratives are produced by interested parties — governments, central banks, corporations, media, propagandists. Narratives are designed to influence perception, manage expectations, advance interests. They are not neutral observations.

Flows are different. Capital flows, trade flows, personnel flows, energy flows, military deployments — these are what actors actually do, not what they say. Flows respond to interests, not to narratives. The discipline of comparing narrative to flows reveals where claimed reality diverges from operational reality.

When narrative and flows align, the situation is what it appears. When they diverge, **the flows are right and the narrative is wrong, or being shaped to mislead**. Identifying the divergence and its source is the highest-value analytical work available.

## Prerequisites

- Access to flow data (financial, trade, capital, personnel — varies by topic)
- Awareness that narratives include official statements, media coverage, and consensus framings
- Discipline to seek flows actively rather than rely on narrative

## Core principle

> Narrative is what they want you to believe. Flows are what they're actually doing. When the two diverge, the flows are usually right — but identifying *why* the narrative diverges from flows is the analytical insight that produces edge.

The mistake is to take narrative as data. It's not. It's positioning. The discipline is to ask "what would I see in flows if the narrative is true vs if it's not?"

## What goes on each side

**Narrative (what's claimed):**
- Official statements (governments, central banks, regulators)
- Corporate communications (press releases, earnings calls, IR materials)
- Media coverage (mainstream consensus framings)
- Expert commentary (think tanks, analysts, prominent voices)
- Public opinion/polling

**Flows (what's actually happening):**
- Capital: who's buying, who's selling, ETF flows, dark pool data, options positioning, futures positioning, FX reserves
- Trade: import/export data, customs records, shipping data, satellite imagery of physical commerce
- Personnel: hiring/firing patterns, talent migration, executive movements, diplomatic personnel changes
- Energy: physical energy flows (pipelines, tankers), reserves changes, refinery activity
- Information: communication patterns, encrypted traffic patterns, public sentiment data
- Military: troop deployments, equipment movements, exercises, satellite-visible activity

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Identify the active narrative

For the topic of analysis, articulate the prevailing narrative:
- What does the official position claim?
- What does mainstream coverage emphasize?
- What does consensus expect?

Be specific. "Things will be fine" is not a narrative. "Inflation will return to 2% by Q4 2026 without recession" is a narrative.

### Step 2 — Identify what flows would confirm the narrative

If the narrative is true, what would we see in flows?
- Capital flowing toward sectors the narrative says will benefit
- Personnel moving to organizations the narrative says will grow
- Trade patterns consistent with claimed economic relationships
- Communication patterns consistent with claimed strategic positioning

### Step 3 — Identify what flows would refute the narrative

If the narrative is false, what would we see?
- Capital quietly fleeing despite reassuring statements
- Talent leaving institutions claiming strength
- Trade patterns inconsistent with claimed economic resilience
- Diplomatic personnel moves inconsistent with claimed alliance strength

### Step 4 — Gather actual flow data

Collect available data on flows. Sources vary by topic:

**Financial flows:**
- CFTC Commitment of Traders reports
- ETF and fund flow databases
- Options open interest and flow
- Treasury TIC data (international capital flows to/from US)
- BIS data (international banking flows)

**Trade flows:**
- Customs data (often weekly or monthly)
- Shipping databases (TradeMap, Lloyd's List, port arrivals)
- Satellite imagery of port activity
- Bilateral trade reports from both directions (often diverge in interesting ways)

**Personnel flows:**
- LinkedIn aggregate data on industry movements
- Corporate filings (executive changes)
- Visa data (where relevant)
- Diplomatic appointment patterns

**Energy flows:**
- Tanker tracking
- Pipeline operational data
- Refinery utilization

**Other:**
- Internet traffic patterns (to/from regions, content patterns)
- Real estate transactions (often reveal capital flight before financial data does)

### Step 5 — Compare narrative to actual flows

For each major claim in the narrative, check against flows:
- Does flow data support the claim?
- Does flow data contradict it?
- Is flow data ambiguous?

### Step 6 — Diagnose divergences

For each significant divergence, hypothesize:
- Why does narrative diverge from flows?
- Who benefits from the narrative being maintained despite contradicting flows?
- What's the time horizon before reality forces narrative correction?

Common patterns:
- Authority maintains narrative for its own legitimacy reasons (central bank claiming everything is fine while privately worried)
- Industry maintains narrative for commercial reasons (companies promoting growth story while insiders sell)
- Government maintains narrative for political reasons (claiming economic strength before election while data weakens)
- Coordinated narrative across multiple sources (often signals deliberate management)

### Step 7 — Cross-country macro spillover analysis (mandatory for satellite economies)

This is the **v2 addition** based on prior methodology gap.

For any analysis of a country, region, or asset class with significant economic dependency on a larger neighbor or system, analysis must include explicit examination of flows in the larger system that affect the smaller.

**Trigger conditions for cross-country macro spillover analysis:**
- Subject is a satellite economy (Belarus / Russia, Mongolia / China, etc.)
- Subject is a peripheral asset class with central dependency (EM debt / US Treasury yields)
- Subject is a regional system with global linkages (Gulf petrostate / oil markets)

**What to check:**

For the larger system the subject depends on:
- Fiscal trajectory (deficit, debt service, monetary financing)
- Monetary policy stance and constraints
- Capital flow patterns
- Currency stability
- Resource flow capacity (energy, food, key commodities)
- Political/regime stability

The subject's outcome is largely **derivative** of the larger system. Analyzing subject without analyzing larger system is the methodology error caught in prior Belarus analysis.

### Step 8 — Translate to forecast

Divergence between narrative and flows is a forecast: when narrative is wrong, narrative will eventually correct. Timing is uncertain but direction is usually clear.

Forecast should specify:
- Direction of expected correction
- Time horizon (with wide confidence interval)
- Mechanism of correction (what events bring narrative into alignment with flows)
- Indicators that would confirm or refute

## Worked examples

### Example 1: BYN/USD case (with v2 cross-country addition)

**Narrative (May 2026):** Belarusian rubble strengthening; managed flexible exchange rate; ЕАБР projection 3.32 average for 2026 baseline.

**Narrative-only forecast (v1 mistake):** weight ЕАБР baseline as central case, project around it.

**v2 mandatory cross-country macro spillover:**

The dominant flow affecting BYN is Russian fiscal/monetary state, not Belarusian factors directly. Required check:

Russian fiscal flows:
- Federal deficit trajectory (4-5 trillion ruble in 2025, widening)
- ФНБ liquid portion drawdown (declining toward critical levels)
- Monetary financing of deficit (yes, accelerating)
- Currency stability mechanisms (administrative measures growing)
- Inflation in Russia (running 8-10% effectively)

Russian capital flows:
- Capital flight ongoing despite restrictions
- Reserve composition shifts (toward gold, away from Western financial assets)
- Trade settlement increasingly in non-Western currencies

Implication for BYN:
- BYN follows RUB with 1-3 month lag
- Russian fiscal stress means RUB pressure persists
- Therefore BYN faces structural pressure regardless of NBRB intervention
- Current BYN strength (April 2026 strengthening) is technical/temporary, not fundamental
- ЕАБР baseline (3.32 average) was probably too optimistic

**Corrected forecast** weighting:
- Stagnation around 3.20-3.40: 35% (was 55% in error)
- Stronger stagnation 3.40-3.80: 30% (was 15%)
- Acute stress 3.80+: 15% (was 5%)
- Stable 2.80-3.10: 20% (was 25%)

The cross-country macro spillover analysis would have caught this gap automatically.

### Example 2: Defense industry narrative vs flows (2024-2025)

**Narrative:** Mainstream financial media largely treated defense as defensive holding, not growth opportunity.

**Flows showed:**
- Smart money positioning long defense throughout 2024
- Pentagon contract awards accelerating (visible in awarding databases)
- Defense talent recruitment intensifying
- European NATO countries quietly increasing defense budgets (visible in budget submissions, not yet in mainstream commentary)
- Weapons consumption rates from Ukraine far exceeding production rates (visible in industry communications)

**Divergence:** narrative undervalued defense; flows showed structural buy.

**Forecast:** defense outperforms despite muted narrative; remained valid until Iran war 2026 made narrative catch up.

**Asymmetric structuring:** This divergence enabled the Rheinmetall-2024 / RTX-2024 type asymmetric bets at moderate prices before narrative consensus formed.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating narrative as data.** Narrative is positioning, not data. Always test against flows.
- **Confirmation flow selection.** Cherry-picking flows that support pre-formed view. Discipline: gather flow data systematically, not selectively.
- **Stale flow data.** Flow data has lag. Acknowledge timestamp. Some flow data updates daily, some monthly, some quarterly.
- **Ignoring why divergence exists.** Identifying divergence without explaining it is incomplete. The "why" is the analytical insight.
- **Skipping cross-country spillover.** This is the v2 mandatory addition. For dependent economies, analysis without checking the system they depend on is structurally incomplete.
- **Single-source flow data.** Multiple sources for important flows; cross-check. Customs data sometimes diverges between countries' reports — divergence is itself signal.

## Integration with deconstruction protocol

This skill is **Layer 8** of the 12-layer protocol — the alpha layer. Always run when the topic involves financial markets, political economy, or any situation where claimed reality diverges from interested parties' actions.

For depth level 2+, mandatory full execution. At depth 0-1, brief check.

For dependent-economy analyses, **cross-country macro spillover is mandatory regardless of depth level**. This is non-optional, post the methodology gap discovered in May 2026.

Output stored in Analysis record under `narrativeVsFlows` field. Major divergences may trigger watchlist additions.

This skill, more than any other, separates ОСП analysis from journalism. Journalism reports the narrative. ОСП checks the narrative against flows, and the divergence is where the value lives.
