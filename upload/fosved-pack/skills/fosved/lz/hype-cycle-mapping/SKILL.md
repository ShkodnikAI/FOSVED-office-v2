# Hype Cycle Mapping — When To Buy, When To Sell, When To Wait

Most technologies follow a recognizable five-stage cycle: an initial breakthrough generates inflated expectations, expectations exceed reality producing disillusionment, but the technology actually matures during the disillusionment phase, eventually reaching productive stability. This pattern, named by Gartner, applies remarkably consistently across technological domains.

Investment outcomes correlate strongly with timing relative to cycle stage. Buying during peak hype produces losses as expectations correct downward. Buying during trough produces gains as the substance matures past the disappointed narrative. Most retail capital arrives at peak; institutional patient capital accumulates during trough.

This is the foundation skill for Лаборатория знаний. Every tracked technology has its hype cycle position assessed first; all other analyses depend on this position.

## Prerequisites

- Specific technology identified (not abstract "AI" but specific application or platform)
- Access to investment, talent flow, narrative, and adoption data for the technology
- Discipline to assess stage from evidence rather than to confirm prior view

## Core principle

> Every technology follows similar stages even if specific details differ. Identifying current stage is more valuable than predicting outcomes — because actions appropriate to each stage differ fundamentally. Investment in technology at wrong stage destroys capital regardless of how good the technology is.

## The five stages

**Stage 1 — Innovation Trigger**
- Initial breakthrough demonstration
- Few commercial implementations exist
- Talent concentrated in research labs and small startups
- Investment limited (early VC, government grants)
- Public awareness minimal
- Time scale: years before reaching this stage from underlying research

**Stage 2 — Peak of Inflated Expectations**
- Media attention explodes
- Investment surges (peak VC funding rounds, public market premium)
- Many startups enter the space; "X for Y" applications proliferate
- Predictions of dramatic transformation common
- Substance often lags claims significantly
- Time scale: 1-3 years from emerging from Trigger

**Stage 3 — Trough of Disillusionment**
- Predictions failing to materialize on stated timelines
- Major implementations disappointing
- Early entrants failing or pivoting
- Investment drying up (companies have trouble raising)
- Public attention shifting elsewhere
- BUT: substantive technology continues developing during this period
- Time scale: 1-3 years duration

**Stage 4 — Slope of Enlightenment**
- Real applications emerging from companies that survived trough
- Best practices becoming established
- Talent reconcentrating around proven approaches
- Investment returns to space (more disciplined now)
- Public narrative more measured
- Time scale: 2-5 years

**Stage 5 — Plateau of Productivity**
- Mature applications widely deployed
- Established players dominate
- Innovation continues but incremental
- Investment characterized by acquisitions, scale-up
- Public treats as established part of landscape
- Time scale: indefinite

## Indicators per stage

For each stage, specific observable indicators:

**Innovation Trigger indicators:**
- Research papers proliferating in specific subfield
- First proof-of-concept commercial implementations
- Specialized conferences forming
- Foundational patents being filed
- Initial seed funding rounds in 2-5 startups

**Peak of Inflated Expectations indicators:**
- Mainstream media coverage frequency increasing
- VC funding rounds at increasing valuations
- Number of companies in space rising rapidly
- Universal application claims ("X will change everything")
- Job postings explicitly requiring the technology
- Existing companies adding the technology to their messaging
- Stock prices of perceived beneficiaries rising disproportionately

**Trough of Disillusionment indicators:**
- High-profile failures or disappointing demos
- Articles questioning original narrative
- Earlier-funded companies struggling, laying off, closing
- Talent emigrating to next-cycle technologies
- Investment in space declining
- "Where did all the X companies go?" articles

**Slope of Enlightenment indicators:**
- Successful production deployments at scale
- Industry standards forming
- Specific use cases becoming clearly defined
- Specialized expertise becoming valuable
- Investment recovering (more disciplined valuations)
- Real revenue replacing speculation in business models

**Plateau of Productivity indicators:**
- Mature ecosystem of vendors
- Standard career paths
- Acquisition activity dominates
- Established companies are the major players
- Public uninterested

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Identify specific technology

Be specific. Not "AI" but "large language model applications for code generation." Not "blockchain" but "smart contract platforms for financial primitives." Specificity is essential because different applications of same underlying technology can be at different stages.

### Step 2 — Gather indicators

Collect observable data for the specific technology across all five stages' indicators. Not all indicators present for every stage; partial pattern is normal.

### Step 3 — Assess primary stage

Based on indicator pattern, identify primary stage. Most technologies are clearly in one stage. Some are in transitions between adjacent stages.

If transition: which direction is movement? Innovation Trigger → Peak is forward; Peak → Trough is also forward. Slope → Plateau is mature transition.

### Step 4 — Cross-check with VC data and talent flow

VC funding patterns and talent migration are strong cross-checks:
- Innovation Trigger: small, specialized VC; talent in specific labs
- Peak: massive funding rounds; talent inflow from adjacent fields
- Trough: VC declining; talent leaving to next-cycle technologies
- Slope: disciplined VC; talent stable
- Plateau: M&A activity; talent established

If VC and talent patterns don't match the apparent stage from media indicators, investigate the divergence — usually indicates either misclassification or imminent transition.

### Step 5 — Identify time horizon to next stage

For current stage, when will transition to next stage likely occur? Specific signals to watch:
- Innovation Trigger → Peak: when does mainstream media discovery happen?
- Peak → Trough: which high-profile failure or disappointment will trigger correction?
- Trough → Slope: which surviving company will demonstrate first profitable deployment?
- Slope → Plateau: when does ecosystem maturity make further breakthroughs incremental?

### Step 6 — Investment implications

Each stage suggests different positioning:
- Innovation Trigger: too early for most investors; specialized early-stage VC only
- Peak: sell narrative if owned; avoid new entry; short opportunity if specifically overpriced
- Trough: accumulate substance; identify survivors; patient capital deploys
- Slope: full deployment; specific company picking matters; growth investing
- Plateau: dividend stage; M&A play; mature company analysis

### Step 7 — Output

Hype cycle position with implications. Format below.

## Worked example — multiple technologies, May 2026

**Technology: Generative AI for enterprise productivity**
Stage: Late Peak / early Trough
Indicators:
- Massive VC funding 2023-2024 with disappointing 2025-2026 outcomes for many funded companies
- "AI fatigue" articles appearing
- Several high-profile enterprise deployment failures
- Talent still flowing in (lagging indicator) but slowing
- BUT: foundational models continue improving; substance underneath narrative
Position: starting Trough — survivors will emerge in 12-24mo
Investment implication: avoid broad AI investments; identify specific surviving companies; prepare to accumulate during Trough deepening

**Technology: Quantum computing**
Stage: Innovation Trigger / early Peak
Indicators:
- Major lab announcements increasing
- First commercial offerings emerging (IBM, Google cloud quantum)
- Specialized VC active but limited
- Mainstream media attention growing
- Specific use case emergence still in early phases
Position: pre-Peak; will likely peak 2027-2029 with first commercial successes triggering hype wave
Investment implication: too early for retail; specialized exposure (foundational patents, talent platforms) reasonable

**Technology: mRNA therapeutic platforms (post-COVID)**
Stage: Late Trough / early Slope
Indicators:
- Initial COVID-driven peak well past
- Many failed clinical trials in 2024-2025 created Trough
- Survivors (Moderna, BioNTech) demonstrating real applications beyond COVID
- Specific successful trials in oncology emerging
- Investment recovering with discipline
Position: Slope of Enlightenment forming
Investment implication: now is accumulation phase; survivors will dominate next decade

**Technology: Lab-grown meat**
Stage: Deep Trough / questioning whether will reach Slope
Indicators:
- Massive 2019-2021 hype with predicted timelines unmet
- Major companies struggling (Eat Just, Memphis Meats issues)
- Cost reduction far slower than projected
- Regulatory complexity persistent
- Talent and capital fleeing
- Question: is this a Trough that will produce Slope, or fundamental viability problem?
Position: Trough but uncertain whether Slope materializes
Investment implication: specialized analysis required; not standard cycle case

## Anti-patterns

- **Conflating different applications of same technology.** AI for coding ≠ AI for image generation ≠ AI for autonomous vehicles. Different stages possible for same underlying tech.
- **Stage inflation by analyst.** Wanting technology to be at favorable stage. Assess from indicators.
- **Single-indicator stage assignment.** Multiple indicators required for confidence. Single indicators often noisy.
- **Confusing scale of funding with stage.** Large funding can occur at multiple stages — Innovation Trigger has small specialized funding, Peak has large hype funding, Slope has disciplined large funding. Distinguishes by other indicators.
- **Assuming all technologies complete the cycle.** Some fail in Trough and don't recover. Lab-grown meat may be example. Cycle is descriptive, not deterministic.
- **Mistaking adjacent technology stages.** AI for code generation may be at Peak; AI for general autonomous agents may still be at Innovation Trigger. Don't conflate.
