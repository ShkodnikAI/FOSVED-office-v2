# Macro Regime Identification — The Map Above the Forecast

Most investment forecasts are wrong not because the analyst missed the specific event, but because the analyst misidentified what regime the world was operating in. Asking "will this stock rise?" without first asking "what regime are we in?" is asking the wrong-order questions.

In an inflationary growth regime, real assets and equities win, bonds lose. In stagflation, commodities and select equities win, most everything else loses. In deflationary recession, cash and Treasuries win, most everything else loses. In disinflationary growth (the rare and prized regime), equities and bonds both win. Asset class returns differ by 30-50% **in the same time period** depending on regime.

This skill is the discipline of always knowing which regime is active and which is approaching.

## Prerequisites

- Access to macro data (CPI, GDP, employment, monetary aggregates, yield curves, commodity prices, dollar index)
- Familiarity with regime taxonomies (Bridgewater All Weather, Dalio's economic machine framework, Hudson Bay's macro model)
- Discipline to update regime identification regularly, not just at major transitions

## Core principle

> You cannot forecast specific outcomes correctly while misidentifying the regime in which they unfold. Regime identification is upstream of all financial forecasting and most political forecasting.

The mistake is to treat regime as a fixed assumption ("we're in normal times") rather than active variable. Regimes change; the same asset class can be a winner in one regime and a loser in the next without the underlying company changing at all.

## The four primary regimes (Dalio framework, simplified)

Two axes: growth (rising/falling) × inflation (rising/falling). Four quadrants:

**Quadrant 1 — Inflationary Growth (rising growth, rising inflation)**
- Real assets win: commodities, real estate, equities of pricing-power companies
- Bonds lose (rising rates compress fixed coupons)
- Cash loses (inflation erodes purchasing power)
- Examples: 1960s late, 2003-2007 commodity supercycle, 2021-2022 post-COVID inflation

**Quadrant 2 — Stagflation (falling growth, rising inflation)**
- Commodities win (driven by supply constraints not demand)
- Gold wins (currency debasement)
- Equities mostly lose (margin compression from costs)
- Bonds lose (sticky inflation prevents rate cuts)
- Cash loses (inflation outpaces returns)
- Examples: 1970s, 2022 selectively, possibly 2026 forming

**Quadrant 3 — Deflationary Recession (falling growth, falling inflation)**
- Long Treasuries win (flight to safety + falling rates)
- Cash wins relatively (deflation increases purchasing power)
- Equities lose (revenue compression, default risk)
- Commodities lose (demand destruction)
- Examples: 2008-2009 acute phase, late 1929, 1997 Asian crisis briefly

**Quadrant 4 — Disinflationary Growth (rising growth, falling inflation)**
- Equities win (revenue growth without margin pressure)
- Bonds win (rates falling toward stable level)
- Real estate wins (cheaper financing + economic growth)
- Cash loses (low rates, decent inflation)
- Commodities mostly lose (no supply pressure)
- Examples: 1990s "Goldilocks", 2014-2019 partially

## Secondary regime characterizations

Beyond the four primary, modern regime analysis adds:

**Debt cycle phase** (Dalio):
- Early: low debt, easy growth
- Middle: rising debt sustainably with growth
- Late: debt rising faster than growth, increasingly fragile
- Blow-off: debt unsustainable, crisis approaching
- Reset: post-crisis restructuring (write-downs, monetary expansion)

US is currently late-stage with characteristics of approaching blow-off.

**Geopolitical regime:**
- Hegemonic stability: clear dominant power, predictable rules
- Contested: multiple powers competing for rule-setting
- Multi-polar transition: order forming around regional powers
- New order forming: post-conflict reorganization

Currently contested transitioning to multi-polar.

**Monetary regime:**
- Hard money (gold standard, asset-backed)
- Fiat with constraint (inflation-targeting, independent central banks)
- Fiat with fiscal dominance (central banks subordinate to fiscal needs)
- Fiat reset (CBDC, financial repression, capital controls)

Currently transitioning from fiat-with-constraint toward fiat-with-fiscal-dominance.

**Technology cycle phase:**
- Innovation: foundational technology emerging
- Scaling: technology reaching mass adoption
- Maturity: incremental improvements, profit consolidation
- Disruption: previous winners losing to new wave

AI currently in scaling phase; legacy IT infrastructure in maturity-becoming-disruption.

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Gather current readings on regime indicators

Standard data: CPI (headline + core), GDP growth (real + nominal), unemployment, wage growth, monetary aggregates (M2 growth), yield curve shape, dollar index, gold price, oil price, commodity index.

For each, current value and trajectory (rising/falling, accelerating/decelerating).

### Step 2 — Classify primary regime

Based on growth and inflation readings, place current state in quadrant. Be specific:
- Q1 (inflationary growth)
- Q2 (stagflation)
- Q3 (deflationary recession)
- Q4 (disinflationary growth)
- Transition: which quadrant moving toward what

### Step 3 — Classify secondary regimes

Position in debt cycle phase, geopolitical regime, monetary regime, technology cycle phase.

### Step 4 — Identify regime instabilities

For each regime classification, identify what would shift it:
- Debt cycle: what events would tip late-stage to blow-off?
- Geopolitical: what events would resolve transition into new order?
- Monetary: what events would force regime shift?

These are the **regime change triggers** — events that would alter the macro context for all forecasts.

### Step 5 — Implications for asset classes

Given current regimes, which asset classes are structurally favored vs disfavored?

This is not a forecast of next month's price — it's structural alignment with the regime. A commodity may rise short-term in a stagflation regime even if specific supply news is bearish; equity may rise in disinflationary growth even with mediocre earnings.

### Step 6 — Implications for ОСП forecasts

Active regime identification feeds into every analysis:
- Forecast scenarios should be consistent with regime
- Probability weights on scenarios should consider regime alignment
- Indicators in I&W watchlist should include regime-shift indicators

If a specific forecast contradicts regime expectations (e.g., predicting equity rally during deepening stagflation), the contradiction must be explicitly addressed — what specific factors override regime?

### Step 7 — Schedule next regime check

**Monthly minimum:** quick regime status update
**On major data releases:** CPI, employment, GDP releases trigger regime re-check
**On regime change:** when transition becomes active, increase to weekly until stabilization
**On regime stress:** when current regime appears unstable, accelerate monitoring

### Step 8 — Output

Regime identification report. Format below.

## Worked example — May 2026 hypothetical

### Current readings:
- CPI headline: 4.1% YoY, decelerating from 5.8% peak (Q3 2025)
- Core CPI: 3.8% YoY, sticky despite headline cooling
- GDP growth: 1.4% Q/Q annualized, decelerating from 2.8%
- Unemployment: 4.6%, rising from 3.6% trough
- Wage growth: 4.2% YoY, sticky
- M2 growth: 1.8% YoY, recovering from negative
- Yield curve: 10Y at 4.6%, 2Y at 4.4% — barely positive after long inversion
- Dollar index: 103, mildly weakening trend
- Gold: $5,180 down from $6,500 peak (March 2026 Iran-related spike)
- Oil: $115 Brent (Iran-elevated, gradually normalizing)

### Primary regime classification:

**Stagflation forming (Q2)**, with deflationary tendencies in some sectors.

Reasoning: growth decelerating (Q3 2025 → present trend), but inflation sticky despite headline cooling. Wages still rising, core inflation still elevated. Iran war contributed temporary commodity shock now normalizing but baseline inflation remains above target.

Not yet full stagflation (would require rising inflation alongside falling growth, currently more like decelerating both).

### Secondary regimes:

**Debt cycle:** late-stage, characteristics of approaching blow-off. US fiscal deficit 7%+ of GDP, debt service approaching 4% of GDP, debt-to-GDP near 130%.

**Geopolitical:** contested → multi-polar transition active phase. Iran war crystallized regional alliance reconfigurations. NATO trust eroded. BRICS+ accelerating alternative arrangements.

**Monetary:** fiat-with-fiscal-dominance forming. Fed faces choice between inflation control (raising rates worsens fiscal position) and fiscal accommodation (allowing inflation). Trajectory toward accommodation.

**Technology cycle:** AI in late scaling, with possible early signs of platform consolidation.

### Regime instabilities:

- Debt cycle blow-off triggers: any acute fiscal event (Treasury auction failure, sudden ratings downgrade, inflation spike forcing rate hikes despite fiscal cost)
- Monetary regime shift triggers: explicit yield curve control announcement, central bank purchases of fiscal-purpose debt
- Geopolitical resolution triggers: any major conflict resolution (Iran ceasefire, Ukraine settlement, China-Taiwan resolution)

### Asset class implications under current regime:

**Favored:**
- Commodities (stagflation tendency)
- Gold (currency debasement, monetary regime shift)
- Selective equities with pricing power (defense, energy, infrastructure)
- Real assets generally
- Foreign currencies vs dollar (selective)

**Disfavored:**
- Long-duration Treasuries (sticky inflation, fiscal pressure)
- Growth equities at high multiples (real rates pressure, margin compression)
- Most consumer discretionary (consumer pressure)

**Selective:**
- Defensive equities (utilities, staples) — neutral, depends on rate trajectory
- Tech equities — bifurcating between AI infrastructure (favored) and legacy (disfavored)
- Emerging markets — depends on dollar trajectory and commodity exposure

### Implications for ongoing ОСП analyses:

Forecasts should:
- Default to stagflation regime assumptions
- Be skeptical of forecasts predicting equity strength without specific catalysts
- Be open to forecasts predicting commodity outperformance (regime-aligned)
- Explicitly address fiscal-dominance risks in any USD-related forecast
- Include monetary regime shift as a non-trivial probability scenario

### Next check:

Monthly regular update. Acceleration triggers:
- Any CPI release showing reacceleration
- Any major Treasury auction with weak demand
- Any Fed action diverging from inflation-targeting framework

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating regime as constant.** "We're in normal times" — there are no normal times. Each period has a specific regime that should be named.
- **Over-precision on regime boundaries.** "Inflationary growth" and "stagflation" aren't binary; transitions are gradual. Acknowledge transitions explicitly.
- **Single-axis classification.** Only looking at growth/inflation while ignoring debt cycle, monetary, geopolitical phases. Modern regime is multi-dimensional.
- **Asset class implications without confidence.** Stating "commodities favored" without acknowledging that regime classification is itself uncertain. Calibrate.
- **Ignoring transitions.** Stable regime gives clear implications; transitioning regime requires monitoring, not point-prediction.
- **Confirmation through asset prices.** "Commodities are rising, must be inflationary growth." Backwards reasoning. Regime should be identified from causal indicators (CPI, growth, debt dynamics), then asset prices interpreted in light of regime.
- **Stuck in last regime.** Most analysts identify regimes correctly when they're stable but miss transitions. Discipline against this requires explicit transition-watching.

## Integration with ОСП

This skill is **continuously active**, not invoked per-analysis. It maintains a current "regime state" that other skills query.

**Used by:**
- Every financial analysis (default regime assumption)
- `decision-tree-forecast` (scenarios calibrated to regime)
- `iw-watchlist` (regime-shift indicators)
- `cross-asset-divergence` (regime-aligned vs divergent moves)
- `asymmetric-bet-structure` (regime-favored vs disfavored asset classes)

**Updated:**
- Monthly regular review
- After major macro data releases
- During transitions: weekly until stabilized

**Stored:** in dedicated `MacroRegime` Prisma model with timestamped entries, allowing historical analysis of regime evolution and ОСП calibration vs regime accuracy over time.

This is a foundation skill — without it, financial forecasts are floating in space. With it, every forecast is anchored in the macro context that determines whether predictions can be right.
