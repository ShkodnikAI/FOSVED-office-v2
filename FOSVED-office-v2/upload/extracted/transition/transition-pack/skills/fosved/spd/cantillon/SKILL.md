# Cantillon Effect — The Order of Money Determines The Distribution of Wealth

Richard Cantillon (~1730) observed that newly created money doesn't flow into all parts of the economy simultaneously. It enters at specific points, benefits the first receivers, then propagates outward — by the time it reaches later receivers, the prices they pay have already risen. The first receivers gain real purchasing power; the last receivers experience inflation as loss.

In modern monetary systems, this effect determines the deep distribution of wealth and power. Central bank emission flows first to commercial banks via QE, primary dealers, and discount window. From there to financial firms, then to large corporations via credit access, then to wealthy individuals via asset price appreciation, eventually to the broader economy via wages and transfers — by which point inflation has eroded the purchasing power of the eventually-arrived money.

Understanding the Cantillon order is understanding the monetary politics underlying everything. **Who gets the new money first?** is the central question of any analysis involving monetary expansion.

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of monetary system mechanics (central bank operations, primary dealers, fractional reserve banking)
- Awareness that "money" includes both narrow money (M1) and broader credit/quasi-money expansion
- For cross-border applications: understanding of global dollar system, satellite-dependent currencies

## Core principle

> When new money is created, it enters at specific points. First receivers gain real purchasing power; last receivers pay inflation as loss. The order of receipt determines wealth and power distribution. Knowing who's first in line is knowing where wealth flows.

The mistake is to treat monetary expansion as uniform — "the money supply grew 10%, prices rose 10%, real economy unchanged." Wrong. The first receivers gained. The last receivers lost. The macro aggregates conceal the redistribution.

## The classical Cantillon order (modern US example)

When Fed creates new reserves (via QE or rate-cutting facilitating credit expansion):

**Tier 1 — Direct first receivers:**
- Primary dealers (~24 large banks with direct Fed access)
- These banks see new reserves first, can lend or invest before prices adjust

**Tier 2 — Bank balance sheets:**
- Banks expand credit, charging interest on newly available funds
- Bank shareholders and executives benefit through expanded balance sheets and profitable lending

**Tier 3 — Financial markets:**
- Investment banks, hedge funds, asset managers receive credit
- Asset prices rise (stocks, bonds, real estate) before consumer prices
- Wealthy individuals holding assets benefit; wealth concentration accelerates

**Tier 4 — Large corporations:**
- Investment-grade corporations access cheap credit
- Stock buybacks, M&A, capital investment
- Executives benefit via stock-based compensation; shareholders benefit via price appreciation

**Tier 5 — Smaller businesses, mortgages:**
- Eventually credit reaches smaller borrowers
- By this time, asset prices already elevated; smaller actors pay higher prices for the same houses, businesses

**Tier 6 — Wage earners:**
- Eventually wages adjust upward (typically with significant lag)
- By this time, prices have risen; wage adjustments often don't fully compensate
- This is where the inflation cost lands

**Tier 7 — Fixed-income and savers:**
- Pay the full cost of inflation
- Real value of savings erodes
- Pensioners on fixed incomes hardest hit

This is the classical case. Real cases involve more complexity (multiple emission channels, varied transmission mechanisms, regulatory effects) but the basic order — financial system first, real economy last — holds.

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Identify the emission event or pattern

For the topic of analysis:
- Is there active monetary expansion? (QE, rate cuts, fiscal monetization, credit expansion)
- Or expansion of credit through other mechanisms (private credit growth, government guarantees, financial innovation)?

### Step 2 — Map the emission channel

How does new money enter the system?
- Central bank facility used (QE bond purchases, repo operations, discount window, FX intervention)
- Direct first receivers (primary dealers, specific banks, sovereign borrowers)
- Channels of further propagation

### Step 3 — Identify each tier of receivers

Map the order of receipt:
- Tier 1 (direct): who actually receives newly created money first?
- Tier 2-N: how does it propagate?
- Final tier: who pays the inflation?

For each tier, identify specific named beneficiaries (companies, individuals, institutions where possible).

### Step 4 — Quantify the wealth transfer

For each tier:
- What value flows to this tier?
- What asset price appreciation happens at this tier?
- What time lag before next tier benefits?

### Step 5 — Identify investment implications

Tier 1-3 receivers benefit most from emission. Investments aligned with these tiers participate in the wealth transfer:
- Bank stocks
- Asset managers and investment banks
- Real assets that financial system bids up
- Companies with cheap-credit access

Avoid tier 6-7 positions (fixed income with low yields, cash holdings that lose to inflation).

### Step 6 — Cross-border Cantillon (v2 mandatory addition)

For analyses of dependent economies and assets, the Cantillon effect of the dominant economy propagates internationally. Specifically:

**Russian Cantillon → Belarus Cantillon (2026 case):**

Russian fiscal-monetary expansion has its own Cantillon order:
- Tier 1: Russian military-industrial complex (war financing flows here first)
- Tier 2: Connected oligarchic banks
- Tier 3: Russian large corporations with state contracts
- Tier 4: Russian real estate and equity holders
- Tier 5: Russian wage earners (with inflation erosion)
- Tier 6: Russian savers and pensioners

But also (cross-border):
- Tier 4 (also): Belarusian exporters to Russia receive ruble revenues — they benefit from emission cycle as ruble values flow to them, then they convert to BYN
- Tier 6 (also): Belarusian wage earners pay Russian-emission-driven inflation through BYN's de facto peg to RUB
- **Belarus as a country sits effectively at Tier 4-5 of Russia's emission cycle**, structurally

This explains why BYN follows RUB with lag — Belarus is not an independent monetary system, it's a peripheral participant in Russia's emission cycle. Domestic Belarusian factors are second-order; the primary driver is Russia's monetary expansion rate and propagation.

**Generalization:** Any small economy substantially dependent on a larger one inherits the larger's emission cycle, typically arriving at Tier 4-5 effectively. Local factors modulate but don't change fundamental position.

Examples:
- CFA franc countries inherit eurozone emission cycle
- Dollarized economies inherit US emission cycle directly
- Yuan-denominated trade increasingly inherits Chinese emission cycle
- Petrostate currencies inherit oil price cycles, which themselves reflect dollar emission

For the analysis: identify the larger emission cycle the subject participates in; place subject in its tier; understand the subject's outcome as derivative.

### Step 7 — Translate to forecast and investment

Cross-border Cantillon has investment implications:
- Subject's wealth concentrates among locals connected to the larger emission cycle (exporters, financial intermediaries)
- Subject's masses pay disproportionately for emission (inflation in their currency)
- Asset prices in subject denomination inflate but real values may not
- Currency of subject deteriorates relative to larger system over time

## Worked example — comprehensive

**Topic:** Belarusian wage earner real wealth trajectory through 2026.

**Domestic Belarusian Cantillon:** Limited domestic monetary expansion; mostly administrative measures.

**Cross-border (Russian) Cantillon:**

Russian emission cycle 2025-2026:
- Tier 1: Defense industry (military spending boom)
- Tier 2: State-connected banks (war financing)
- Tier 3: Large state-owned enterprises
- Tier 4: Belarusian exporters to Russia who receive ruble flows (small but present beneficiaries in Belarus)
- Tier 5: Belarusian general economy participants (no direct benefit, exposed to RUB pass-through to BYN)
- Tier 6: Belarusian wage earners (BYN inflation, eventually wage compensation with lag)
- Tier 7: Belarusian savers and pensioners (full inflation cost)

**Forecast for Belarusian wage earner:**
- Real wage trajectory negative: emission cycle places them at Tier 6, inflation erodes purchasing power
- Russia's emission rate determines pace of erosion
- Belarusian fiscal/monetary independence is constrained — cannot escape larger cycle
- Investment implication: real assets (housing in stable areas, gold, hard currency) preserve purchasing power; BYN-denominated assets do not

**Forecast for Belarusian exporter to Russia:**
- Beneficiary of emission via ruble revenue flows
- BYN conversion preserves some value
- Real wealth growing despite inflation
- Investment implication: long position in companies producing goods Russia needs

This level of analysis is impossible without explicit Cantillon framing and specifically the cross-border extension.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating money supply as uniform.** Macro statistics conceal Cantillon redistribution. Specific actors benefit; specific actors pay.
- **Ignoring time lags.** Cantillon effects play out over months and years, not days. Short-term observations miss the pattern.
- **Single-tier focus.** Looking only at Tier 1 (banks getting QE) without tracing through real economy. Distribution effect is in the propagation pattern.
- **Assuming inflation distributes evenly.** Inflation is paid most by Tier 6-7 actors. Aggregate inflation rate hides who actually pays.
- **Skipping cross-border analysis.** v2 mandatory for dependent economies. Without cross-border Cantillon, satellite economy analysis is fundamentally incomplete.
- **Confirmation bias on emission's effects.** "QE benefits Wall Street at Main Street's expense" is true and provable through Cantillon analysis but should be derived from evidence, not assumed.
