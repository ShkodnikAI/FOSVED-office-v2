# Forces & Potentials — From Catalog to Vector Mechanics

The old Layer 9 (Beneficiaries) listed who wins and who loses. This is necessary but not sufficient. Knowing that Russia benefits from controlling Belarus tells us nothing about whether that control will increase, hold, or decrease.

The mechanics of any process are **vector mechanics**. Each force has a magnitude and a direction. Multiple forces sum into a net vector. The subject moves in the direction of the net vector, at a speed proportional to its magnitude.

This skill brings physics into analysis. Not as metaphor — as operational discipline.

## Prerequisites

- `awareness-frame` completed (forces inward and outward already named)
- 12-layer deconstruction completed or in progress (provides factual data per force)
- `cantillon` skill loaded if monetary flows are involved
- `narrative-vs-flows` completed (Layer 8 — distinguishes claimed forces from actual ones)

## Core principle

> Process direction = vector sum of force potentials. Force potential = ambition × resources × strategic-task-clarity × leverage. A force without one of these factors has zero contribution regardless of how loudly it claims importance.

The discipline is not "list the forces and discuss them." The discipline is **multiplication**: any force missing one component (no resources despite ambition, no leverage despite resources, no strategic clarity despite leverage) contributes zero or near-zero. This filters narrative-loud forces from actually-effective ones.

## The four-component potential formula

For each force, evaluate four components on a 1-10 scale, then multiply:

**Ambition (A): How badly does this force want this outcome?**
- 1: outcome is irrelevant to force's interests
- 5: outcome is one of several priorities
- 10: outcome is existential or core mission

Sources: stated objectives, historical pattern of investment in this issue, internal documents (if available), psychology of decision-makers.

**Resources (R): What can this force actually deploy?**
- 1: minimal — symbolic only
- 5: moderate — can sustain pressure but not decisive
- 10: overwhelming — can force outcome unilaterally

Includes: financial, military, diplomatic, technological, human capital, time horizon (long-time-horizon force has more effective resources than equally-funded short-time-horizon force).

**Strategic-task clarity (S): Does this force have a coherent plan for this outcome?**
- 1: chaotic, contradictory actions
- 5: directionally coherent but improvising
- 10: detailed multi-stage plan with backup paths

A force with chaotic strategy wastes its ambition and resources. Hitler's Russia campaign had high A and R but moderate S (overstretched, internal Nazi politics) — and S degraded over time.

**Leverage (L): Can this force translate ambition + resources + strategy into actual effect on the outcome?**
- 1: no effective channels to influence outcome
- 5: indirect channels that work slowly
- 10: direct decisive channels

A force with high A, R, S but low L is a tiger in a cage. North Korea has high A and S regarding Korean reunification, moderate R, but low L outside its immediate periphery.

**Potential (P) = A × R × S × L × E**

where E is the **environmental modifier** (1.0 = healthy substrate; <1.0 = depleting; >1.0 = abundant new resources flowing in).

This multiplication matters. A force scoring 10/10/10/2 has nominal potential 2000. A force scoring 5/5/5/5 has nominal potential 625. The first is more effective despite the second having higher average. Multiplication punishes weakness in any one dimension.

The **environmental modifier** is critical for honest analysis. A force can score high on A, R, S, L on paper, but if its substrate is depleting (running out of capital, talent leaving, social legitimacy eroding), its effective potential degrades over time. Russia in 2022 had high A, R, S, L on paper for its operation in Ukraine; its E was 0.85 and falling. By 2026 nominal R is similar but E has degraded to ~0.65 (war drag, sanctions accumulation, talent emigration, fiscal stress). Effective potential = nominal × E.

Conversely, a force with abundant new substrate can have E > 1.0 — China's E in 2010-2020 was 1.2-1.4, with new capital flows, expanding institutional capacity, and growing talent base amplifying its other components.

**Scoring E:**
- E = 1.5: rapidly expanding substrate, surplus resources available for force's mission
- E = 1.0: substrate stable, all components functioning normally
- E = 0.85: depleting but functional — can sustain current operations 1-3 years
- E = 0.65: substantially depleted — visible strain on force's operations
- E = 0.40: critically depleted — force operating on inertia, system-health-diagnostics urgently required
- E = 0.20: collapse imminent or active

E is forward-looking. Even if current performance is normal, depleting substrate means future degradation. This catches cases where forces look powerful but are hollowing out internally.

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Identify all relevant forces

From awareness frame and deconstruction layers (especially Layer 9 expanded), list all forces with material influence on the subject. Typical count: 4-8 forces. Less than 3 = incomplete analysis. More than 10 = too granular, consolidate.

For each force, define:
- Identity (specific actor or coalition, not abstraction)
- Position relative to the outcome (pro / anti / sideways)
- Time horizon of action (short / medium / long)

### Step 2 — Score each component for each force

For each force, score A, R, S, L on 1-10 scale. Document **briefly why** each score (single phrase). This is critical — scores must be defensible, not pulled from intuition.

If insufficient data to score a component for a force, that's a finding — flag with `?` and note what data would resolve it. Don't fake confidence.

### Step 3 — Compute potential

For each force: P = A × R × S × L. Range: 1 to 10,000.

Categorize:
- **Dominant** (P ≥ 5000): force shapes the outcome
- **Major** (P 1000-4999): force materially affects outcome
- **Active** (P 100-999): force has some effect
- **Marginal** (P < 100): force claims influence but lacks substance

### Step 4 — Map vectors

Lay forces on a vector diagram:
- Direction: pro / anti / orthogonal to subject's interests
- Magnitude: proportional to potential
- Time horizon: now / 6mo / 12mo / 36mo

### Step 5 — Compute differential

For the central question (will outcome X happen?):

- Sum potentials of forces pushing toward outcome X: P_pro
- Sum potentials of forces pushing away from outcome X: P_anti
- Differential: D = P_pro - P_anti

Sign of D = direction of process. Magnitude of D relative to (P_pro + P_anti) = velocity.

If |D| / (P_pro + P_anti) > 0.5: process moves decisively in direction of D
If 0.2 - 0.5: process moves in direction of D but with possibility of stalling or reversal
If < 0.2: contested, near-equilibrium, small inputs can flip direction

### Step 6 — Account for third-party forces

Third forces can distort the differential without being clearly pro or anti the central question. Common patterns:

**Distortion**: third force changes the available resources of one of the main forces (e.g., Ukraine front reduces Russia's effective resources for Belarus pressure)

**Redirection**: third force changes the strategic priorities of one of main forces (e.g., China's growing involvement in Belarus changes Russia's calculation)

**Acceleration**: third force creates time pressure on one main force, changing their behavior

For each third force, compute its effective contribution to D as **modulation** of one of the main forces, not as separate addition.

### Step 7 — Evolution over time

Compute differential at multiple time horizons:
- D_now (current state)
- D_6mo (likely state in 6 months given current trajectory of force components)
- D_12mo
- D_36mo

A trajectory matters as much as current state. A subject where D_now is small but trending strongly negative is in a different position than one where D_now is large but stable.

## Worked example

**Question:** Will Russian control over Belarus's economic policy increase, hold, or decrease through end of 2026?

**Forces:**

| Force | Position | A | R | S | L | E | P | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Russia (compress Belarus) | Pro | 9 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 0.65 | 2293 | War-drag reduces R from 9 to 7; E reflects substrate depletion (sanctions, talent loss, fiscal stress) |
| Belarusian elite (resist absorption) | Anti | 4 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 0.85 | 61 | Lukashenko personal, no successor consensus, weak; substrate depleting (institutional decay) |
| Ukrainian war (third — modulates Russia) | Distorts | -2 to Russian R | | | | | | Reduces Russia P by ~30% currently |
| EU sanctions on Belarus (third — modulates Russian leverage) | Distorts | -1 to Russian L | | | | | | Slight reduction |
| China (third — provides alternative to Russia) | Distorts | +0.15 to Belarus E | | | | | | Modest substrate boost, growing |
| Belarusian population FX preference (anti-instability) | Tactical anti | 5 | 4 | 4 | 2 | 0.90 | 144 | Acts on currency, not directly on policy control |

**Differential calculation:**
- P_pro (Russian control increases): 2293
- P_anti (Russian control resists): 61 + 144 = 205
- D = 2293 - 205 = 2088
- |D| / (P_pro + P_anti) = 2088 / 2498 = **0.84**

Still decisive (anything >0.5 is decisive trajectory). But notice — without the E modifier, Russia's nominal P would have been 3528 and the picture would look more dominant than it actually is. The environmental modifier captures the real degradation that war-drag and sanctions are imposing.

**Time evolution with E projection:**
- Russia E_now: 0.65
- Russia E_12mo: 0.55-0.60 (war continues, fiscal stress increases)
- Russia E_36mo: 0.50-0.70 (depends on war outcome)
- Belarus E_now: 0.85
- Belarus E_12mo: 0.80 (China substrate adds, but internal decay continues)

Effective Russia P degrades faster than Belarus P, reducing the differential over time. By 36 months, if war goes against Russia, D could fall to 0.55-0.65 — still decisive but materially closer to contested.

**Conclusion:** Russian compression of Belarus continues with high probability through 12 months. Material trajectory shift requires either Russian war outcome to deteriorate substantially, or Belarus substrate to expand significantly via China — both possible over 24-36 months but neither imminent.

## Anti-patterns

- **Adding scores instead of multiplying.** A + R + S + L treats absent components as just "lower averages." Multiplying punishes weakness in any one component, which is correct — a force without leverage is impotent regardless of ambition.
- **Including narrative forces as if they had potential.** A loud advocacy group with no resources, leverage, or strategic clarity claims influence but has near-zero P. Don't inflate their score because they're loud.
- **Treating third forces as separate vectors.** Third forces modulate main forces. Adding them as separate equal-weight vectors double-counts their effect.
- **Fixed scores without time horizon.** Force potentials change over time. War drag reduces Russian R over months. Score current and project to horizons.
- **Faking confidence on missing data.** If you can't score component, mark `?` and note what you'd need. Better to have flagged uncertainty than fake precision.
- **Pure mechanical reading.** D = 0.85 means "decisive trajectory" — but it doesn't predict every step. Subject can still take maneuvers (see `strategic-maneuvers` skill) that delay or redirect even a strong differential. Forces & potentials gives the underlying current; maneuvers operate within that current.

## Integration with deconstruction and forecast

Forces & potentials replaces the old "Beneficiaries (2x2 matrix)" in Layer 9 of deconstruction. The matrix listed actors; potentials measures them.

This skill is **upstream of `decision-tree-forecast` and `strategic-maneuvers`**. The forecast must be consistent with the differential — if the forecast claims subject moves direction X but D points the other way, this is a red flag requiring reconciliation. Either the forecast is wrong (more likely) or the potentials missed a force (rarer but possible).

Strategic maneuvers operate **within** the potential field — they don't change the potential of forces, but they can change their **vectors** through smart positioning. See `strategic-maneuvers` for the maneuver catalog.

Output is archived in Analysis record under `forcesAndPotentials` field. Verification at horizon date checks: did D_now correctly predict subject's movement direction? If yes, calibration improves. If no, error category is logged (was it wrong scoring? missed force? missed time evolution?).
