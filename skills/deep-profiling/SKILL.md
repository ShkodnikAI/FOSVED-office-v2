# Deep Profiling — Open-Source Intelligence on Decision-Makers

The leaders making decisions you analyze are not abstractions. They are specific humans with biographies, psychologies, relationships, fears, ambitions, health, financial pressures, ideological commitments, and personal histories that shape every decision they make. Most analysis treats them as black boxes — "the Fed will decide based on data." Deep profiling treats them as humans whose specific characteristics predict specific decisions.

For high-stakes ОСП analyses, this skill provides what intelligence agencies call "leadership analysis" — systematic profiling using only publicly available information sources, but used at depth that most analysts skip.

## Prerequisites

- Subject is a **public figure** with significant decision-making power affecting the analyzed topic
- Analysis depth level 3+ (full intelligence operation)
- Owner authorization for full mode (depth 4) accessing dark web/leaked databases
- Discipline that this is professional analysis, not gossip

## Core principle

> Decisions are made by humans, not institutions. Understanding the human predicts decisions better than understanding the institution. Open sources contain enough information about most public figures to construct meaningful profiles when the analyst has the discipline to gather and synthesize.

The mistake is to treat decision-makers as rational utility-maximizers responding to data. They aren't. They respond to data filtered through their psychology, biography, relationships, current physical state, ideological commitments, financial pressures, and ego. The professional profiler maps these filters.

## What the profile contains

**1. Biographical foundation**
- Origin: family background, formative environments
- Education: institutions, mentors, intellectual influences
- Career: pattern of choices, who promoted them, who they rose through
- Significant life events: traumas, transformations, public failures and recoveries

**2. Psychological characteristics**
- Risk tolerance (revealed through past decisions, especially under pressure)
- Decision-making style (deliberative vs intuitive; consensus vs unilateral)
- Stress response (visible in past crises)
- Ego structure (status-seeking vs achievement-seeking; vulnerability to flattery vs criticism)
- Cognitive style (analytical, experiential, ideological)

**3. Network**
- Mentors and benefactors (who helped them rise; what they owe)
- Allies (who they protect, who protects them)
- Adversaries (who they fight, what motivates the fight)
- Family (who depends on them; who has leverage)
- Personal relationships (whose opinion matters)

**4. Current state**
- Physical: signs of health, aging, fatigue, illness from public appearances
- Psychological: signs of stress, change in cognitive sharpness, emotional regulation
- Financial: known wealth, investments, financial pressures
- Political: standing within own faction, threats to position, succession concerns

**5. Leverage points and vulnerabilities**
- What they fear losing
- What weaknesses adversaries could exploit
- What pressures could shift their decisions
- What they would never compromise on (red lines)

**6. Recent communications**
- Patterns in public statements
- Changes in tone, vocabulary, register over time
- Body language patterns from video appearances
- Comparison: what they say publicly vs what their actions show they believe

## Source categories

**Default mode (open web):**
- Biographical: Wikipedia, biographies, journalism profiles, official statements
- Network: org charts, meeting records, photo archives, attendance lists
- Public statements: official communications, interviews, social media
- Video: speeches, press conferences, candid moments at public events
- Financial: required disclosures (politicians, executives), SEC filings, real estate records
- Photos: public events showing physical state, body language, who they're with

**Escalated mode (added):**
- Leaked communications (confirmed-authenticity leaks; not unverified rumors)
- Whistleblower-published documents
- Dark web discussions (as data, not as reliable source)
- Specialized databases (insider transaction patterns, PoliticalMoney databases, NGO research)

**Full mode (rarely used, owner-approved):**
- Deep social network mapping including non-public connections inferred from data patterns
- Pattern-of-life analysis from publicly available metadata
- Combined database analysis revealing what individual sources don't show

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Define purpose and scope

What ОСП analysis requires this profiling? Specific questions:
- What decisions by this person matter for the analysis?
- What time horizon for those decisions?
- What aspects of profile are most relevant (psychology, health, network, leverage)?

Avoid scope creep — profile what's relevant, not everything possible.

### Step 2 — Biographical foundation

Build chronological timeline of major life events. Most public figures have published biographical material; gather and cross-reference.

For each major transition (education choices, career moves, significant events):
- What was happening externally (context)
- What choices they made
- What this revealed about character

### Step 3 — Network mapping

Identify:
- Mentors who promoted them
- Lieutenants they brought along
- Adversaries (overt and covert)
- Family members with influence
- Personal relationships affecting decisions

Build network map. Look for: who has leverage, whose opinion they consult, who would defect under stress.

### Step 4 — Communication pattern analysis

Collect significant public statements over time. Analyze:
- Vocabulary shifts (do they use new framings? old ones falling away?)
- Tonal shifts (more confident? more defensive? more aggressive?)
- Topic emphasis (what they talk about reveals priorities)
- Topic avoidance (what they don't talk about reveals concerns)

For non-English speakers, original language analysis matters — translations often miss nuance.

### Step 5 — Video analysis

For accessible public figures, video record analysis reveals:

**Physical state:**
- Aging vs prior video records (faster than calendar = stress or illness)
- Signs of impairment: tremor, slurred speech, gait changes, weight changes
- Energy level: alertness, response time, vocal strength

**Psychological state:**
- Microexpressions (Ekman framework — universal facial expressions revealing emotion under control)
- Body language patterns (open vs defensive postures; comfort vs stress signals)
- Vocal patterns (cadence changes, hesitations, vocabulary degradation)

**Specific signals:**
- Slurred speech or unusual word substitutions: cognitive concerns
- Persistent tremor (not stress-related): neurological concerns
- Sudden weight changes: serious illness possibility
- Reduced public appearances: declining health, internal opposition, or strategic concealment

### Step 6 — Identify leverage and vulnerabilities

Based on biography + psychology + network + current state, identify:
- What they fear losing (status, family, wealth, legacy, freedom)
- Who has leverage over them (financial, political, personal)
- What pressure points adversaries could exploit
- What ideological or personal commitments they would not compromise on

This is most sensitive part of profile. Used analytically, not for action against the subject.

### Step 7 — Predict decision tendencies

For the original ОСП analysis question, predict:
- How will this person likely react to scenario A vs B?
- What might surprise them?
- What pressures might shift their decision?
- What would change if they faced specific events (health, family, political shock)?

### Step 8 — Validate against history

Test predictions against historical decisions: when faced with situations similar to currently anticipated, how did this person actually decide? Track record reveals more than self-presentation.

## Worked example — abbreviated

**Subject:** Hypothetical analysis of a major central banker's likely policy direction.

**Biographical foundation:**
- Origin: middle-class academic family
- Education: top economics PhD, Keynesian-tradition mentors
- Career: academic → research bank → policy. Promoted by establishment figures.
- Significant events: was deputy during 2008 crisis (formative experience showing willingness to act unconventionally)

**Network:**
- Mentors include figures who emphasized institutional preservation
- Allies span both parties; bipartisan respect
- Adversaries: hawks who consider him too dovish

**Communication patterns:**
- Vocabulary increasingly uses "data dependent" and "patient" — signals reluctance to commit
- Topic emphasis shifted from inflation in 2022 to financial stability in 2024-25
- Avoidance: doesn't discuss fiscal-monetary coordination explicitly

**Video analysis:**
- Recent appearances show maintained energy but more careful word choice
- Some signs of stress under aggressive questioning (more pauses, more "let me address that")
- Physical state: stable, no concerning signs

**Leverage and vulnerabilities:**
- Fears losing: institutional credibility, legacy, retirement reputation
- Leverage: political pressure visible but not acted on; will resist visible capitulation
- Red line: institutional independence (would resign rather than compromise on appearance)

**Decision tendencies:**
- Bias toward gradualism and consensus
- More willing to act unconventionally during crisis (2008 lesson)
- Sensitive to historical comparisons (Volcker, Greenspan)
- Likely path: defer hard choices via "data dependent" framing while quietly accommodating; willing to overshoot if crisis emerges

**Implication for ОСП forecast:**
- Probability of dovish surprise: medium (history suggests can act if crisis frame appears)
- Probability of hawkish surprise: low (against character)
- Probability of inertia/delay: high (matches consensus, deflects political pressure, preserves optionality)

## Anti-patterns

- **Profiling private individuals.** This skill is for public figures with decision-making power. Profiling private citizens is not professional analysis.
- **Replacing analysis with profiling.** Profile informs analysis; doesn't replace it. A leader's psychology is one input, not the only one.
- **Reading psychology from single signals.** "He looked tired in that video, must be ill" — single-signal claims unreliable. Pattern across multiple appearances over time required.
- **Confirmation bias in profiling.** Wanting to find weakness in disliked figures, strength in liked ones. Discipline against this is essential.
- **Stereotyping by category.** "All [group] leaders behave this way" — individuals vary within categories. Profile the individual, not the category.
- **Hindsight psychology.** Reading post-decision behavior into pre-decision profile. Profile from contemporaneous evidence only.
- **Action recommendations.** This skill produces analysis, not action. "We should pressure them on X" is owner's decision, not analyst's output.
- **Privacy violations in pursuit of profile.** Even for public figures, certain personal information (health, family in some cases) raises legitimate boundaries. Default mode covers most needs.

## Integration with ОСП

This skill is **Tier 2 with depth-trigger**:
- Loaded automatically at depth level 3+
- Loaded for any analysis where named decision-maker has material impact on outcomes
- Available on-demand via `/profile <subject>` command

**Output stored in:** `Profile` Prisma model. Profiles are versioned (subjects evolve over time; their state is snapshot-and-update).

**Periodic update:**
- High-stakes subjects (Fed chair, key political leaders): quarterly profile update
- Medium-relevance subjects: annual update
- Low-relevance subjects: profile-on-demand only

This skill is one of the most powerful in the toolkit because it accesses analysis dimensions most observers ignore. The cost (research time) is real; the value (decisions predicted before the system understands them) compensates substantially for major analyses.
