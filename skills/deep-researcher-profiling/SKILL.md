# Deep Researcher Profiling — Reading The People Whose Work Drives Technology

Major technological developments come from specific researchers whose work is identifiable, traceable, and predictive. The mRNA vaccine breakthroughs trace to specific researchers (Karikó, Weissman, others). AlphaFold traces to specific team. Most foundational technologies have specific human authors whose work over decades led to the breakthrough.

This skill identifies these key researchers in current emerging technologies, profiles them through public sources, and extracts intelligence about technological trajectory from understanding their work patterns. Unlike the broader `deep-profiling` skill (focused on decision-makers and political figures), this targets technical talent.

## Prerequisites

- Specific technology of interest with identifiable research community
- Time investment for academic literature search and network analysis
- Familiarity with academic publishing and research network norms

## Core principle

> Major technological breakthroughs come from specific researchers whose careers, networks, and work patterns are observable. Profiling key researchers reveals technological trajectory more accurately than corporate announcements — researchers' research direction is the actual leading indicator of where capability comes from.

## What to track

**Academic background:**
- Where they trained (PhD, postdoc)
- Who their mentors were
- What intellectual lineage they're in
- Specific subfield specialization

**Publication patterns:**
- What they publish and where
- Co-author networks (who they collaborate with)
- Citation patterns (whose work they build on)
- Recent shift in topic (signal of new direction)

**Career trajectory:**
- Industry vs academia
- Moves between organizations (signal of priorities)
- Founding of companies
- Advisory roles

**Network position:**
- Central vs peripheral in their subfield
- Specific connections that matter
- Mentoring/advising patterns

**Recent activity:**
- Conference attendance and talks
- Public statements (interviews, blogs)
- Patents filed
- New collaborations

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Identify key researchers in subfield

For specific technology, identify researchers whose work matters:
- Authors of foundational papers
- Authors of recent breakthrough papers
- Highly-cited researchers in subfield
- Founders of leading startups
- Researchers at top labs working on subfield

Aim for 5-15 key researchers per subfield analysis.

### Step 2 — Build researcher profiles

For each, gather from public sources:

**Academic background:**
- Google Scholar profile
- LinkedIn
- University faculty pages
- ORCID

**Publication patterns:**
- Recent publications (last 3-5 years)
- Citation networks
- Co-author networks

**Career trajectory:**
- Position changes
- Company founding/advisory
- Notable transitions

**Recent activity:**
- Conference talks and slides
- Public interviews
- Blog posts or social media (if professional accounts)
- Patent filings

### Step 3 — Identify networks and clusters

Researchers often cluster:
- By academic lineage (same advisor)
- By collaboration networks
- By organizational affiliation
- By specific technical approach

Map clusters. Cluster movement (researchers from one cluster joining another) is significant.

### Step 4 — Identify trajectory shifts

For each researcher, what's the trajectory?
- Stable in subfield (continuing developing)
- Shifting subfields (signal of new direction)
- Moving between academia and industry
- Founding companies

Recent shifts are most significant.

### Step 5 — Extract intelligence about technology direction

What do researchers' patterns reveal?
- Where is foundational work happening (which labs, which approaches)?
- What approaches are gaining momentum (more researchers joining)?
- What approaches are declining (researchers leaving)?
- What's happening at boundaries between subfields (cross-domain potential)?

### Step 6 — Identify investment-relevant patterns

For ОСП analyses:
- Which startups have key researchers?
- Which corporate labs have key researchers?
- Where are research clusters most concentrated?
- What new companies might emerge from current researcher activity?

### Step 7 — Output

Researcher profile summary with implications.

**Subfield:** AI applied to biology / structural biology / drug discovery

**Key researchers identified:**

- Background: physics/computational biology PhD
- Recent: AlphaFold-2/3, now leading Isomorphic Labs research
- Trajectory: from research scientist to leader; commercial focus increasing
- Network: extensive collaboration with structural biology community
- Implication: structural biology AI moving rapidly to commercial application; Isomorphic Labs commercial direction strong

**Demis Hassabis (DeepMind/Isomorphic):**
- Background: neuroscience PhD, AI background
- Recent: leading both DeepMind and Isomorphic; also drug discovery direction
- Trajectory: increasingly entrepreneurial; Isomorphic commercial direction
- Network: top-tier across AI and biology
- Implication: leadership-level commitment to AI-biology commercialization

- Background: deep RL research

- Trajectory: shift from one frontier lab to competitor — signal about lab direction
- Network: extensive in AI safety, RL community
- Implication: senior talent migration suggests Anthropic prioritizing certain capabilities; OpenAI may be losing edge in specific areas

**Specific biology-AI startup founders:**
- Multiple founders from major labs starting biology-AI startups
- Pattern: lab researchers → founder pattern in 2024-2025
- Implication: wave of founder-led companies emerging from frontier labs

**Cluster analysis:**
- DeepMind/Isomorphic cluster: dominant in structural biology AI
- Stanford/MIT clusters: significant academic centers

**Investment implications:**

## Worked example — quantum computing researchers

**Subfield:** Quantum computing for practical applications

**Key researchers:**
- Multiple at IBM Research (Sergei Kovalev, others — illustrative)
- Multiple at Google Quantum AI
- Researchers at startups (Atom Computing, IonQ, PsiQuantum)
- Academic centers: MIT, Harvard, Caltech leading

**Patterns:**
- Talent flowing into industry from academia (different from earlier era when academia dominated)
- Specific researchers founding companies after key publications
- Cross-pollination between physics-AI groups (interesting pattern)

**Investment implications:**
- Industry concentrating capability; bets on industry leaders make sense
- Specific companies with founding researcher teams more credible than those without
- Cross-domain (physics-AI) suggests longer-term opportunities at intersection

## Anti-patterns

- **Hero worship.** Single researcher attribution misses team dynamics. Most major work is collaborative.
- **Old data.** Publication and career data ages. Recent activity matters most for current direction.
- **Domain narrowness.** Sometimes important researchers are in adjacent fields. Don't constrain too tightly.
- **Ignoring industry researchers.** Public information about industry researchers can be limited. Don't underestimate them just because less visible.
- **Citation count fetishism.** High-citation researchers are important but recent work matters more for trajectory than past work.
- **Network confirmation.** Networks often reflect old patterns. New patterns emerging from less-prominent researchers can be missed.
- **Privacy violations.** Even for public researchers, certain information has reasonable boundaries. Default to professional information.
