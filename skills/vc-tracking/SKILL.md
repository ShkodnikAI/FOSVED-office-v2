# VC Tracking — Following The Smart Money

Venture capital is in business of betting on technological futures. Sophisticated VCs spend their lives evaluating emerging technologies, talking to researchers, watching trends. When they collectively concentrate capital in a specific subfield, they're sharing signal about what they believe will work.

VC tracking isn't following hype — it's specifically watching where sophisticated capital concentrates with discipline. Not every VC investment is signal; the patterns of investment by quality investors across sustained periods reveal direction.

This skill is the methodology for extracting that signal from continuous funding flow data.

## Prerequisites

- Access to VC funding data (Crunchbase, Pitchbook, public databases)
- Familiarity with VC quality differentiation (which firms have track records)
- Discipline against following pure-hype investments

## Core principle

> Sophisticated capital following discipline reveals technological direction. Quality of investors matters more than quantity of investment. Sustained concentration matters more than single rounds. VC tracking distinguishes signal from noise through these qualifiers.

## What to track

**Funding rounds:**
- Round size (Series A, B, C, growth)
- Round amount
- Lead investor
- Participating investors
- Valuation
- Date

**Investor patterns:**
- Which investors are most active in specific space
- Track records of those investors in similar prior cycles
- Investment thesis disclosed publicly
- Portfolio concentration

**Aggregate flows:**
- Total funding by subfield over time
- Number of rounds
- Geographic distribution
- Stage distribution

## Investor quality differentiation

Not all VC firms are equal in signal value. Tiers of quality:

**Tier 1 — Highest signal:**
- Firms with multiple historical successes in similar emerging tech
- Firms with deep domain expertise (specific partner backgrounds)
- Firms that disclose differentiated thesis
- Examples: Founders Fund, Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz at top of list, with subfield-specific specialists in second rank

**Tier 2 — Moderate signal:**
- Established firms without specific domain expertise
- Corporate venture arms with strategic angle
- Geographic specialists in regional emergence

**Tier 3 — Low signal:**
- Late-stage growth investors (chasing momentum, not direction)
- Crossover funds (often hype-driven)
- Family offices without disciplined approach

For analytical purposes, Tier 1 investments carry most signal weight; Tier 2 supporting; Tier 3 often noise.

## Patterns that signal

**Concentration by Tier 1 investors:**
When multiple Tier 1 firms invest in same subfield within same period — strong signal of viability.

**Sequential validation:**
When Tier 1 firm leads round, then more join in subsequent rounds — validation deepening.

**Insider buying:**
When existing investors increase positions in follow-on rounds at higher valuations — strong commitment signal.

**Founder migration patterns:**
When founders from successful Tier 1 portfolio companies start new ventures, those ventures attract attention.

**Corporate strategic VC:**
When major industry participants invest strategically — different signal than financial VC. Indicates strategic relevance more than purely financial returns.

## Patterns that warn

**Late-stage pile-on:**
When Tier 3 investors arrive at peak valuations after Tier 1 already invested earlier — often near peak hype.

**Universal participation:**
When everyone is investing in space — suggests crowded, late stage.

**Valuation inflation without revenue:**
Ratios diverging from comparable past cycles signals froth.

**Down rounds across portfolio:**
Multiple companies in space accepting lower valuations than prior rounds — signals loss of momentum.

**VC departure from space:**
Tier 1 firms publicly stating they're stepping back from a previously-active subfield — significant negative signal.

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Define subfield to track

Specific is better:
- "Generative AI startups" (broad)
- "Generative AI for code generation" (narrower)
- "Foundation model training infrastructure" (specific)
- "Drug discovery using ML" (specific)

### Step 2 — Establish data sources

- Crunchbase or Pitchbook subscription for systematic data
- VC firm websites and disclosures
- Press releases and announcements
- Specialized industry trackers

### Step 3 — Collect funding data

For chosen subfield over relevant period (typically 12-36 months):
- All rounds with details (amount, investors, date)
- Aggregate by quarter or month
- Focus on rounds >$10M for primary signal; smaller rounds as supporting

### Step 4 — Filter by quality

Apply investor tier weighting:
- Tier 1 investments: full weight
- Tier 2 investments: half weight
- Tier 3 investments: low weight (often noise)

### Step 5 — Identify patterns

Look for:
- Concentration of quality investors in specific narrow areas
- Sequential validation (multiple rounds with quality investors)
- Founder patterns (alumni networks)
- Strategic vs financial investor ratios

### Step 6 — Cross-reference with other intelligence

Patterns make most sense alongside:
- Talent flow (where are people going?)
- Patent activity (what are companies building?)
- Public announcements (what's claimed?)

Convergent signals strengthen interpretation; divergent signals require investigation.

### Step 7 — Generate intelligence findings

What does VC pattern reveal?
- Subfields receiving disciplined commitment
- Subfields receiving hype-only investment (caution flag)
- Specific bets that are emerging consensus among smart money
- Specific bets that smart money is avoiding

### Step 8 — Output

VC tracking summary with implications.

## Worked example — AI infrastructure VC tracking (illustrative, 2024-2025)

**Subfield:** AI infrastructure (training, inference, observability)

**Funding data:**

**Quality Tier 1 investments:**
- Multiple Sequoia, a16z, Founders Fund investments in foundation model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, others)
- Specialized infrastructure rounds (Together AI, Fireworks, others) with Tier 1 leads
- Inference-specific startups (Groq, Cerebras) with mixed Tier 1/specialized

**Strategic VC participation:**
- NVIDIA strategic investments substantial (multiple portfolio companies)
- Microsoft, Google, Amazon strategic involvement

**Concentration patterns:**
- Foundation models: well-funded with quality investors
- Inference infrastructure: high investor interest
- Observability/safety: emerging interest, Tier 1 investments starting
- Specialized hardware: substantial but few clear leaders

**Sequential validation:**
- Anthropic raising successive rounds at growing valuations from Tier 1 — strong commitment
- Specific infrastructure companies completing follow-on rounds with original Tier 1 leads — sustained commitment

**Patterns:**
- Substantial sustained Tier 1 commitment to AI infrastructure
- Strategic VC participation indicates real industry relevance, not pure financial bet
- Some areas (foundation models) crowded; others (specialized hardware) more selective

**Cross-references:**
- Talent flow: matching — top engineers going to these companies
- Patent activity: matching — substantial patent flow in same areas
- Public momentum: substantial

**Findings:**
- AI infrastructure represents serious sustained commitment from sophisticated capital
- Foundation models specifically: high concentration, will see consolidation
- Inference: race for who provides best inference economics
- Specialized hardware: opportunity for specific bets

**ОСП implications:**
- Investment thesis: position in foundational picks (NVIDIA), diversified exposure to infrastructure plays
- Watchlist: track which infrastructure companies survive consolidation
- Asymmetric structure: specific infrastructure companies pre-IPO have asymmetric upside if survive

## Anti-patterns

- **Following crowd VC.** Tier 3 piling on at peak prices. Recognizes hype, not opportunity.
- **Ignoring quality differentiation.** Treating all VC equally. Quality matters more than quantity.
- **Single-round overreaction.** "Sequoia invested $50M" — interesting but pattern matters, not single round.
- **Late-stage focus.** Late-stage rounds reveal less than early-stage. Series A/B more predictive of direction.
- **Geographic blind spots.** US-focused VC tracking misses Chinese, European patterns.
- **Confusing financial VC with strategic.** Strategic VC has different motivations and signals different things. Don't conflate.
- **Hype-period overconfidence.** During hype peaks, even Tier 1 investors sometimes invest below their usual discipline. Cross-check with other signals.
