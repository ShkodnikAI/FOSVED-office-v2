# Cycle Position — Where Are We In The Long Waves

History rhymes through cycles. Not perfect repetition, but recognizable patterns repeating at different scales — short business cycles, longer credit cycles, generational power shifts, century-scale geopolitical reorganizations. Identifying the current position within these cycles constrains what's possible (and what's likely) in ways that point-prediction analysis cannot.

A bull market in late-stage debt cycle is different from a bull market in early debt cycle, even with identical short-term metrics. A geopolitical crisis during hegemonic transition resolves differently than the same crisis during stable hegemony.

This skill is the discipline of always knowing where on the wave we are.

## Prerequisites

- Familiarity with cycle frameworks (Dalio, Kondratieff, Juglar, Strauss-Howe generational)
- Long historical data perspective (cycles operate on multi-decade timescales)
- Discipline against hindsight bias (cycles are clearer in retrospect)

## Core principle

> Same short-term data point means different things at different cycle positions. Strategies that work in one phase fail in another. The cycle position constrains what's possible; ignoring it produces forecasts that look reasonable but are systematically wrong because they assume the wrong context.

## The cycles to track

**Long debt cycle (Dalio, ~75-100 years):**
- Early: low debt, productive growth
- Middle: rising debt sustainably with growth
- Late: debt rising faster than growth, increasingly fragile
- Blow-off: unsustainable, crisis approaching
- Reset: post-crisis restructuring (writedowns, monetary expansion)

Current position (US, 2026): late-stage with characteristics approaching blow-off. Debt-to-GDP near 130%, debt service growing, fiscal deficit 7%+ structural. Reset characteristics include monetary expansion, financial repression, currency reform.

**Kondratieff long wave (~50-60 years):**
Technology-driven cycle of innovation → diffusion → maturity → crisis → new innovation.

Current position: in transition between mature internet/digital wave and emerging AI/biotech wave. Late stage of one cycle, early stage of another.

**Geopolitical hegemonic cycle (~80-100 years):**
- Hegemonic stability (clear dominant power)
- Challenge (rising power)
- Transition (active conflict over rules)
- New order (post-transition stability)

Current position: transition phase. Post-1945 Pax Americana destabilizing; multipolar order forming.

**Juglar cycle (~7-11 years):**
Business cycle of investment-driven expansion and contraction.

Current position: tracking via standard business cycle indicators.

**Kitchin cycle (~3-5 years):**
Inventory-driven shorter cycle.

Current position: tracking via inventory and capex data.

**Generational cycle (Strauss-Howe, ~80 years):**
Four-generation pattern of social-political mood.

Current position: in "fourth turning" / crisis era according to this framework. Coincidence or signal?

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Establish current position in each major cycle

For each cycle:
- What evidence supports current position classification?
- What's the trajectory (how fast moving through phase)?
- When (roughly) did we enter current phase?
- What characteristics will mark transition to next phase?

### Step 2 — Identify cycle-aligned vs cycle-discordant patterns

Some current observations align with cycle position (expected). Others don't (discordant signals).

Discordant signals are diagnostic — they suggest either:
- Cycle position is misidentified
- Local non-cycle factors dominating
- Approaching transition

### Step 3 — Apply cycle constraints to forecasts

For ОСП forecasts in active analyses:
- Which scenarios are cycle-aligned (more probable given cycle position)?
- Which are cycle-discordant (less probable; require special explanation)?
- What does cycle position suggest about timing of likely events?

### Step 4 — Watch for cycle transitions

Transitions between phases are critical inflection points. Indicators of approaching transition:
- Late-cycle excesses become extreme (debt cycle: euphoria, debt to record highs, leverage at peaks)
- Symptoms of stress in cycle-supporting structures (debt cycle: monetary policy effectiveness declining, central bank balance sheets expanding)
- Catalyst events that previous cycle position absorbed without disruption now produce disproportionate response

### Step 5 — Update periodically

- Quarterly: full cycle position review
- Annually: major reassessment of cycle frameworks themselves
- Event-driven: when major events suggest possible transition

## Worked example — May 2026 cycle position

**Long debt cycle (US):**
Position: late-stage, characteristics of approaching blow-off
Evidence: debt-to-GDP 130%, structural deficit 7%+, debt service approaching 4% of GDP, monetary policy losing effectiveness (high rates haven't crushed growth as expected, low rates haven't restored productive investment), increasing reliance on extraordinary measures
Trajectory: deteriorating; transition to reset phase plausible within 5-15 years
Transition markers to watch: Treasury auction stress, monetary financing of deficits, capital controls, currency reform discussions

**Kondratieff long wave:**
Position: end of digital/internet wave (1970s innovation, 2000s mass adoption, 2020s maturity), beginning of AI/biotech wave (2020s innovation, 2030s mass adoption likely)
Evidence: digital/internet productivity gains plateau; AI productivity gains beginning; biotech regulatory and commercial maturation visible
Trajectory: in active transition; both old and new waves visible

**Geopolitical hegemonic:**
Position: transition phase
Evidence: US-China economic decoupling active, BRICS+ alternative arrangements forming, NATO unity stressed (Iran war 2026 revealed European reluctance), regional powers asserting (Saudi-Israel, Turkey, India)
Trajectory: continuing transition; new order not yet stable

**Juglar:**
Position: late expansion approaching potential contraction
Evidence: capex deceleration, employment plateau, yield curve inversion history
Trajectory: contraction within 12-24mo plausible

**Kitchin:**
Position: late phase of inventory cycle
Evidence: inventory-to-sales ratios elevated, manufacturing PMI weakening

**Generational:**
Position: "fourth turning" / crisis era
Evidence: institutional distrust at multi-decade highs, generational political alignment shifting, willingness to consider previously-unthinkable policies

### Cycle alignment for current ОСП analyses:

**Stagflation forecast:** cycle-aligned — late debt cycle typically produces stagflation as productive growth weakens but fiscal spending continues. Probability appropriately weighted.

**Geopolitical instability:** cycle-aligned — transitions produce conflict. Iran war 2026 fits pattern; further conflicts probable.

**Defense industrial expansion:** cycle-aligned — both geopolitical transition and late debt cycle (which often resolves through war or war-equivalent fiscal expansion) support defense growth.

**AI/biotech disruption:** cycle-aligned — Kondratieff transition phase historically produces foundational technology innovations.

**Discordant signal:** US equity multiples remain elevated despite late-cycle indicators. This is partly cycle-aligned (late-cycle euphoria) but extreme in scope. Watch for cycle-position confirmation through eventual mean reversion.

### Implications for ОСП forecasts:

- Default toward cycle-aligned scenarios (higher probability weight)
- Be skeptical of cycle-discordant predictions (require strong specific evidence)
- Watch for transition indicators (Treasury stress, AI breakthrough events, geopolitical realignments)
- Time horizons should match cycle scales (debt cycle transitions occur over years, not months)

## Anti-patterns

- **Cycle determinism.** "It must happen because cycles say so" — cycles are tendencies, not certainties. Specific events can violate cycle expectations.
- **Single-cycle analysis.** Looking only at debt cycle while ignoring geopolitical or technology cycles. Multi-cycle integration produces better forecasts.
- **Hindsight cycle classification.** Easy to identify cycle phases in retrospect; hard prospectively. Discipline of operating with current evidence only.
- **Confusing scale.** Mistaking Kitchin cycle position for Juglar position, or short-term volatility for long-cycle transition. Each cycle operates on its own timescale.
- **Cycle-discordant signal dismissal.** "That's just an outlier" — discordant signals are diagnostic. Investigate before dismissing.
- **Ignoring time scales.** Predicting precise dates from cycle analysis. Cycles work on rough scales; precision is false confidence.

## Integration

Updated **quarterly** as default; **immediately** when major events suggest transition.

Used by:
- `decision-tree-forecast` (cycle-alignment of scenarios)
- `macro-regime-id` (cycle position is one input to regime)
- `iw-watchlist` (cycle transition indicators are watchlist entries)
- All forecast skills (cycle context default for time horizon assumptions)

Stored in `CyclePosition` Prisma model with timestamped entries enabling historical analysis of cycle position changes.

This skill anchors ОСП in long-term context. Without it, forecasts can be technically correct about short-term dynamics while being systematically wrong about what those dynamics mean for outcomes that play out over years.
