# Two Paths Synthesis — Formal Logic Meets Game Theory

A single analytical method produces a single blind spot. Pure formal-logic analysis assumes opponents are mechanical — they will do the rational thing given their potentials. Pure game-theoretic analysis assumes opponents are perfect strategists — they will use deception, take risks, surprise. Both are wrong for the same reason: they're complete answers from incomplete frameworks.

The discipline of two paths: run both independently, then compare. Where they agree, you have triangulation — high confidence. Where they disagree, the disagreement is the diagnostic — it points to either where the formal model misses behavior, or where the game-theoretic model overestimates strategic capacity, or to a real strategic frontier where outcomes truly are contested.

## Prerequisites

- `forces-and-potentials` completed (potentials are measured)
- `strategic-maneuvers` completed (maneuver options are catalogued)
- Subject and opponent are clearly identified
- Time horizon for the analysis is set

## Core principle

> Convergence is signal. Divergence is signal. Both are signal — but different kinds.

The mistake is to treat divergence as "ambiguity" or "uncertainty" and shrug. Divergence is **information**: it tells you exactly where to look harder, where the situation is genuinely open, where small inputs can change outcomes.

## The two paths

### Path A — Formal Logic (mechanical)

**Approach:** Apply forces-and-potentials output mechanically. The differential D points the direction. The forecast is the mechanical extrapolation of current vectors and their projected trajectories. Treat all actors as pursuing their stated/measured potentials in the most straightforward way.

**Key features:**
- No surprise moves
- No deception or bluffs
- Each force pursues its rational objective with available resources
- Behavior consistent with historical patterns
- Outcomes determined by vector sum

**Output:** "Given current potentials and trajectories, the most likely outcome is X with probability P, Y with probability Q, Z with probability R."

This is the engineer's analysis. It's correct most of the time. The mistake is to think it's correct all the time.

### Path B — Game Theoretic (strategic)

**Approach:** Model each major actor as having its own analytical apparatus, its own awareness of the situation, and capacity for unexpected moves. Ask:
- What is opponent thinking right now?
- What does opponent know about the subject's position?
- What deceptions might opponent be running?
- What unexpected moves are within opponent's capacity?
- What does opponent know that the subject doesn't?

**Key features:**
- Opponents are intelligent agents with their own analysis
- Deception, bluffs, and surprise are possible
- Information asymmetry is central
- Reflexivity matters — opponent knows that subject is watching, and adjusts
- Outcomes can deviate from mechanical predictions

**Output:** "Given strategic possibilities, opponent might [unexpected move]. If they do, outcome shifts to X' with probability P'. Watch for [signals] that would indicate opponent is positioning for unexpected move."

This is the chess player's analysis. It accounts for what the engineer's analysis cannot — that the world contains intelligent adversaries.

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Run Path A formally

Take the differential D from `forces-and-potentials`. Without considering deception or surprise, project:

- What is the direct mechanical outcome at each time horizon (now, 6mo, 12mo, 36mo)?
- What is the velocity? (How fast is subject moving in direction of D?)
- What sequence of intermediate states leads from now to projected outcome?
- What probability range is appropriate given uncertainty in component scores (not deception)?

Document Path A output as a clean projection.

### Step 2 — Run Path B by adopting opponent's mind

For each major opposing force, mentally adopt their position:

**Sub-step 2a — Opponent's analytical capacity.** What analysis do they have? What's their internal staff doing? Major states have their own intelligence apparatus, comparable in some areas to subject's. Major corporations have analyst teams. Skilled individuals have intuition shaped by experience. Don't assume opponent is naive.

**Sub-step 2b — Opponent's awareness of subject.** What does opponent see? What does opponent know about subject's potentials, vulnerabilities, leverage points? Opponent is also doing forces-and-potentials analysis on subject — what would their version look like?

**Sub-step 2c — Opponent's strategic options.** What maneuvers are available to opponent (apply `strategic-maneuvers` skill from opponent's perspective)? Which would they prefer given their character, time horizon, risk tolerance?

**Sub-step 2d — Opponent's deception and bluffs.** What might opponent want subject to believe that isn't true? What signals is opponent sending that might be noise designed to mislead?

**Sub-step 2e — Opponent's unexpected moves.** What's the move opponent could make that would surprise — not random but calculated to break subject's expected path? Black-swan-from-opponent-side.

Document Path B output with multiple scenarios incorporating these strategic possibilities.

### Step 3 — Compare paths

Place outputs side by side. For each major dimension of the analysis, ask: do the two paths agree?

**Convergent points:** both paths predict same outcome. This is high confidence.

**Divergent points:** paths predict different outcomes. Either:
- (a) Path A is right and Path B is over-strategizing (opponent is actually more mechanical than strategic)
- (b) Path B is right and Path A is missing strategic dimension
- (c) Genuinely contested point — the situation is at a strategic frontier where outcomes are open

### Step 4 — Diagnose divergences

For each significant divergence, attempt diagnosis:

**Test for (a) — opponent is mechanical:**
- Has opponent shown capacity for strategic moves historically? If no, lean toward Path A.
- Does opponent have time and resources for sophisticated strategy? If constrained, mechanical behavior more likely.

**Test for (b) — Path A misses strategic:**
- Are there specific signals (in deconstruction layers) that suggest opponent is positioning for surprise? Recent unusual moves, increased activity in unexpected areas, changes in communication patterns.
- Does opponent have pattern of using deception in similar situations historically?

**Test for (c) — genuinely contested:**
- Both diagnoses fail. The situation is open. This is the most actionable finding — you know exactly which question matters.

### Step 5 — Synthesize the joint output

Final synthesis combines:

**High-confidence predictions:** points where Path A and Path B converge — present these as the most reliable predictions with high probability.

**Conditional predictions:** points where outcome depends on opponent's strategic choice — present these with the strategic conditions that would activate each branch.

**Frontier points:** genuinely contested — present as "the question that matters most." These often become the basis for I&W watchlist entries because they're where the most signal will appear.

### Step 6 — Translate to forecast

Pass synthesis output forward to `decision-tree-forecast`. The decision tree uses convergence points as anchors, divergence points as branching nodes.

## Worked example

Subject: Russian compression of Belarus through end of 2026.

### Path A (Formal):

D = 0.81 (decisive Russian). Mechanical projection:
- Russian pressure on Belarus continues at current intensity through 2026
- BYN/USD trajectory follows ЕАБР projection toward 3.32 average
- Lukashenko regime stable formally; succession positioning continues
- Probability of regime change: ~10% over 12mo, ~25% over 36mo

### Path B (Game-theoretic):

Adopting Russian perspective:
- Russia knows Belarus has limited options
- Russia's analytical apparatus likely sees same D ~0.85
- Russia's strategic option: accelerate compression while war continues to drag, lock in Belarus before Russia's recovery from war makes Belarus more confident
- Possible surprise move: engineered transition (replace Lukashenko with Russian-aligned successor) before Lukashenko's natural decline opens unpredictability

Adopting Belarusian elite perspective:
- They see same D
- Their option: position for soft-landing — alliance with non-Russian creditors, especially China
- Possible surprise: collective elite move to stabilize Krutoy or similar successor with quiet Chinese backstop, presenting Russia with fait accompli

### Comparison:

**Convergent:** Russian compression continues through 2026 — high confidence.
**Convergent:** BYN/USD trajectory broadly tracks ЕАБР baseline if Russian war state doesn't change.

**Divergent:** Path A says regime change probability ~10% in 12mo. Path B suggests if Russia accelerates engineered transition or if Belarusian elite stabilizes alternative succession, regime "change" (in form) probability is 15-25% even within 12mo.

**Diagnosis of divergence:** This is a frontier point. Russia has historical pattern of preferring stability over engineered transitions (favors mechanical Path A). But Russian war stress increases incentive for accelerated moves (favors Path B). Watch for signals: Krutoy positioning, Russian fiscal escalation, Chinese investment in Belarus.

**Synthesis:**
- High confidence: Russian economic pressure continues, BYN trajectory follows ЕАБР baseline mid-range
- Conditional: regime form/succession outcome depends on whether Russia-Belarus elite-China dynamics resolve via Path A (stability) or Path B (engineered transition)
- Frontier: watch Krutoy positioning + Chinese investment data + Russian fiscal indicators in Q3 2026

This synthesis has more actionable content than either path alone. Path A alone says "stable, probability 10%." Path B alone says "watch for surprise." Two paths synthesis says "stable on economic axis with high confidence; succession axis is contested with specific watchpoints."

## Anti-patterns

- **Running both paths simultaneously instead of sequentially.** They contaminate each other. Run Path A clean first, document. Then run Path B clean. Then compare.
- **Assuming convergence means certainty.** Convergence means high confidence relative to either path alone, not absolute certainty. Black swans (events outside both models) still happen.
- **Faking Path B.** Adopting opponent's mind requires actual effort to model their analytical capacity, awareness, options. Skipping this and writing "opponent might surprise" without specifics is theater, not analysis.
- **Ignoring divergences.** "We can't tell which path is right, so we'll average them" — wrong. Divergences are information. Diagnose them. If diagnosis fails, frontier points are themselves a finding.
- **Path B as fallback when Path A is uncomfortable.** Subject has unfavorable potentials → analyst hopes Path B will save them by predicting opponent makes mistake. This is wishful Path B, not real Path B. Real Path B accepts that opponents are usually smart.
- **No specific watchpoints.** Divergence/frontier points must produce specific signals to watch for. "Watch for surprises" is useless. "Watch Krutoy meeting frequency with Chinese officials" is useful.

## Output template

```
─── TWO PATHS SYNTHESIS ───

Path A (Formal Logic):
  Mechanical projection: <outcome with probability>
  Time horizon: <projection at now / 6mo / 12mo / 36mo>
  Velocity: <how fast>
  Probability range: <given uncertainty>
  Key assumption: opponents pursue rational objectives mechanically

Path B (Game Theoretic):
  Adopting [Opponent 1] mind:
    Their analytical view: <what they see>
    Their preferred maneuvers: <list>
    Possible surprise: <specific>
  Adopting [Opponent 2] mind:
    [same structure]
  Modified projection: <outcome accounting for strategic moves>

CONVERGENT POINTS (high confidence):
- <Prediction 1>
- <Prediction 2>

DIVERGENT POINTS:
- <Prediction X>: Path A says <Y>, Path B says <Z>
  Diagnosis: [(a) Path A correct | (b) Path B correct | (c) frontier]
  If frontier: watchpoints — <specific signals>
- [more divergences]

SYNTHESIS:
High confidence: <list>
Conditional: <list with conditions>
Frontier: <list with watchpoints>
```

## Integration with deconstruction protocol

This skill is the third operation in **Phase 3 (Staff Block)**, after `forces-and-potentials` (measure) and `strategic-maneuvers` (options for subject). Pattern:

```
Phase 3a: Forces measured
Phase 3b: Goals & drivers explicit
Phase 3c: Strategic maneuvers for subject generated
Phase 3d: Two paths synthesis ← this skill
```

Output of two paths feeds directly into **Phase 4 (decision-tree-forecast)**:
- Convergent points become the trunk of the decision tree
- Divergent/frontier points become branching nodes
- Watchpoints become indicators on the tree

For ОСП v2, this is the skill that transforms intelligence analysis into **strategic intelligence** — the kind that actually informs decisions under adversarial conditions.

Output is stored in Analysis record under `twoPaths` field. Verification at horizon date checks: did convergent points hold? At divergent points, which path proved right? This is **the most valuable feedback** for calibration — it tells us whether our Path A or Path B mental modeling is well-calibrated against reality.
