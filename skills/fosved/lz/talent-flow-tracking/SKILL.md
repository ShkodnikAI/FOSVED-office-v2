# Talent Flow Tracking — Following The People Who Build The Future

Strategic announcements are public and managed. Talent decisions are public but unmanaged — researchers and engineers move based on opportunity, money, mission, lifestyle, and the move is publicly visible through LinkedIn, professional networks, and conference appearances.

When top talent moves, they bring expertise. When critical mass of talent concentrates somewhere, capability follows. When key talent leaves an organization, that organization loses capability whether or not they admit it.

This skill is the discipline of treating talent flow as primary intelligence about technological direction — leading indicator more reliable than corporate announcements.

## Prerequisites

- Identified domain or organizations to track
- Access to LinkedIn, professional databases, conference attendance lists
- Discipline to distinguish significant from routine moves

## Core principle

> Talent migrations are leading indicators of capability shifts. Top talent goes where mission, money, and opportunity align. Where critical mass concentrates, capability follows in 2-5 years. Tracking talent flow reveals strategic direction before announcements.

## What to track

**Movements of key individuals:**
- Top researchers (specific named experts in field)
- Senior engineers (architects of key systems)
- Founders of relevant startups
- Key executives in specific technical roles

**Movements of teams:**
- Group migrations (multiple people from same source going same direction)
- Spinout patterns (when teams leave to start new ventures)
- Consolidation patterns (when multiple teams join same organization)

**Patterns over time:**
- Net inflow vs outflow per organization
- Quality trajectory (are top talent arriving or leaving?)
- Cross-domain transfers (biology talent to AI labs, finance talent to tech)

## What's significant vs routine

**Significant moves:**
- Recognized leaders (well-known in field)
- Specialists in narrow expertise (rare skills)
- Group moves (signal organizational decision)
- Cross-domain moves (signal new direction)
- Senior-to-startup moves (signal opportunity recognition)

**Routine moves:**
- Junior staff
- Lateral moves between similar roles
- Single-person moves with obvious explanations (geography, family)

Focus on significant moves; routine moves are noise.

## The procedure

### Step 1 — Define tracking scope

What domain or organizations? Specific is better:
- "AI/ML researchers at top labs" (broad)
- "Biology-AI specialists at top labs" (narrower)
- "Computer vision specialists" (specific)
- "DeepMind alumni" (organization-specific)
- "Founder migrations from FAANG" (pattern-specific)

### Step 2 — Establish baseline talent map

For chosen scope, who's where currently?
- Major researchers in field with current affiliations
- Key team members at relevant organizations
- Recent notable hires/departures

This is the baseline against which changes are measured.

### Step 3 — Monitor for changes

Sources for monitoring:
- LinkedIn (direct profile changes)
- Conference attendance (changes in affiliation)
- Paper authorship patterns (who's publishing for whom)
- Press releases and announcements (direct reporting)
- Specialized industry trackers (TheInformation, Substack writers in specific fields)

Cadence: monthly for active monitoring; weekly during high-activity periods.

### Step 4 — Categorize each move

For each tracked move, categorize:
- Significance: senior/specialist/group vs routine
- Direction: from where to where
- Stated reason: how move is publicly framed
- Inferred reason: what move likely actually means
- Implied capability shift: what does this tell us about the source and destination

### Step 5 — Identify patterns

Aggregate across moves:
- Is one organization gaining talent disproportionately?
- Is one losing talent?
- Is talent flowing toward specific subfield?
- Are founders concentrating in specific areas?

### Step 6 — Cross-reference with other intelligence

Patterns make most sense alongside:
- Patent activity (does patent direction match talent direction?)
- Public announcements (what's claimed vs what's implied)
- Investment patterns (where is money going?)
- Technical progress (which areas advancing fast?)

### Step 7 — Generate intelligence findings

What does talent flow reveal?
- Capability shifts in progress
- Strategic priorities of major actors
- Emerging fields gaining serious commitment
- Declining areas losing serious commitment

### Step 8 — Output

Talent flow intelligence summary.

## Worked example — AI biology talent flow (illustrative, 2024-2025)

**Tracking scope:** Biology-AI specialists movements between research labs.

**Significant moves observed:**

**To DeepMind/Isomorphic Labs:**
- Multiple senior structural biology researchers
- Drug discovery specialists from pharma
- Computational chemistry experts

Pattern: DeepMind/Isomorphic substantially expanding biology capability beyond AlphaFold.

**To OpenAI:**
- Limited biology hires
- Some biology-adjacent (RLHF specialists with diverse domain backgrounds)

Pattern: OpenAI not prioritizing biology heavily compared to general AI.

**To Anthropic:**
- Some biology research
- Limited dedicated biology team

Pattern: Anthropic similar to OpenAI but smaller scale.

**To biotech startups:**
- Multiple startups in protein design
- Some in drug discovery using AI
- Founder pattern: AI lab alumni starting biology-focused companies

Pattern: significant founder migration to AI-applied-to-biology startups.

**From academia:**
- Strong outflow from top biology programs to AI labs
- Especially structural biology, drug discovery, protein engineering

Pattern: capability shifting from academia to industry, with industry substantially expanding capability.

**Cross-references:**
- Patent activity: matches — DeepMind/Isomorphic increasing biology patents
- Investment: AI-biology startups receiving substantial VC funding
- Technical progress: AlphaFold-2/3 demonstrated viability; recent advances in protein design

**Findings:**
- AI applied to biology entering commercial-development phase
- Major lab commitment is real (DeepMind/Isomorphic specifically)
- Founder pattern indicates wave of startups coming
- Timeline: 2025-2028 for first major commercial successes

**ОСП implications:**
- Investment thesis: long position in foundational AI providers (DeepMind via Google, NVIDIA for compute), plus diversified exposure to AI-biology startups
- Watchlist: track top startups — SaaS Bio, Recursion, Schrödinger evolution, plus emerging protein design startups
- Risk assessment: regulatory pathway for AI-discovered drugs is uncertain; some startups will fail

## Worked example — China AI talent flow (illustrative)

**Tracking scope:** AI talent flow involving China-based organizations.

**Pattern observations:**

**Inflow from Western academia/labs:**
- Reverse brain drain — Chinese-origin researchers returning
- Specific reasons: geopolitical concerns, opportunity, family
- Magnitude: meaningful but not dominant

**Outflow from China-based labs:**
- Emigration to US, Europe, Singapore
- Specific reasons: regulation, opportunity, geopolitical
- Magnitude: ongoing

**Net flow:**
- Roughly balanced — China not losing talent net but not gaining significantly net either
- Quality of inflow vs outflow uncertain

**Within China:**
- Concentration around DeepSeek, Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent
- Movement between Chinese labs frequent

**Findings:**
- China retains substantial talent base
- Net flow not dominantly favoring either side
- Capability differential vs US not changing rapidly through talent flow alone (compute is bigger differential)
- Specific area: open-source AI talent concentrated in Chinese labs (DeepSeek being notable)

**Implications for technology-OOB analysis:**
- Talent component scoring: roughly stable
- Doesn't indicate dramatic shift in race dynamics from talent alone
- Other factors (compute, capital, regulation) more determinative

## Anti-patterns

- **Conflating routine with significant moves.** Many moves are normal. Focus on signal, not noise.
- **Single-move generalizations.** "X joined Y, must be a major shift" — usually overinterpretation. Patterns matter, not individual moves.
- **Overlooking team/group moves.** Team moves much more significant than individual moves. Watch for coordinated migrations.
- **Confirmation bias in interpretation.** Wanting talent flow to confirm prior beliefs about who's "winning." Track objectively.
- **Ignoring cross-domain talent.** Talent moving between domains is most significant signal — biology people joining AI labs is bigger signal than AI people moving between AI labs.
- **Static talent maps.** Talent maps need continuous updating. Quarterly minimum for active tracking.
